🍽️ More Than a Meal: How You Eat Matters

When I was growing up, my family shared a home-cooked dinner every night. We'd catch up on each other's days and make plans. Scarcely a dinner went by without a guest joining us, adding to the fun. I now prioritize dinnertime with my own family. Setting cellphones aside, we strengthen our connection over a meal.

📖 Vocabulary - Paragraph 1

Catch up on
To learn the latest news or do something you haven't had time to do
Spanish: Ponerse al día
"I need to catch up on my emails after vacation."
Scarcely
Hardly, barely, only just
Spanish: Apenas, raramente
"She had scarcely finished speaking when the phone rang."
Aside
To one side; away from the main position
Spanish: A un lado
"She set her phone aside during the meeting."

Studies confirm that shared family meals have physical and emotional benefits for children and youth. It reduces unhealthy eating habits, eating disorders, substance use and depression. The benefits of communal eating aren't just confined to family meals. A University of Oxford study found that people who place an emphasis on eating with others tend to feel happier and less alone. They feel better connected to their community and experience greater life satisfaction than those who usually eat alone.

Yet, modern culture has shifted away from communal meals and toward eating alone as people move farther from home, live alone and work at jobs with varying hours. According to Psychology Today, Americans are eating nearly half of their meals alone! East Asian countries like Japan and Korea are also experiencing this trend toward eating solo.

Does this mean there are no benefits to solo dining? Not at all! While communal dining has health benefits, there are positive aspects to eating alone as well. For some, eating alone helps them recharge and reduce stress after being in a socially demanding environment all day. There is also more personal choice and independence when eating alone with no need to consider others' food needs or preferences.

To get the full benefits of eating alone, distractions must be set aside. Without conversation or digital screens capturing your attention, the flavors, smells and textures of the food become more enjoyable. Quiet moments of eating with nothing else to distract you can also spark creativity. Rarely does a good idea become apparent to a person without the brain having the time and freedom to wander. Quietly eating a meal is an opportunity for this.

Eating with others and dining alone both have value. Shared meals build connection, while solo meals offer space to recharge. The key is being present. So, the next time you sit down to eat, turn off your screen, slow down and enjoy the moment — whether you're with other people or on your own.

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

+ 1. What does "catch up on" mean? Can you use it in a sentence about your work?
Answer: "Catch up on" means to learn the latest news or do something you haven't had time to do.

Example: "After my vacation, I need to catch up on all the orders that came in while I was away."

Work context: "Let me catch up on the meeting notes before we start the discussion."
+ 2. According to the article, what are TWO benefits of eating alone?
Answer: The article mentions several benefits:

1. Helps recharge and reduce stress - especially after being in a socially demanding environment all day

2. More personal choice and independence - no need to consider others' food needs or preferences

3. Better appreciation of food - without distractions, you can enjoy flavors, smells, and textures more

4. Sparks creativity - quiet moments allow your brain time to wander and generate ideas
+ 3. How has modern culture "shifted away from" communal meals? Give examples from the article.
Answer: Modern culture has shifted away from communal meals because:

• People move farther from home (away from family)
• Many people live alone
• Jobs have varying hours (not everyone eats at the same time)

Result: Americans eat nearly half their meals alone, and this trend is also happening in East Asian countries like Japan and Korea.
+ 4. What is the main message of the article? Do you agree with it?
Main message: Both eating with others and eating alone have value. The key is being present and mindful - turning off screens and enjoying the moment, whether you're with people or alone.

Key takeaway: Shared meals build connection, while solo meals offer space to recharge. Both are important for different reasons.

Personal reflection: Think about your own eating habits. Do you take time to be present during meals, or are you usually distracted by your phone or TV?
