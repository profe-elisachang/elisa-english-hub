Article

From Screens to Stores: The Power of Viral Foods
— Harry Jenkins
Key word Phrasal verb Idiom / expression

If you're on social media, you've probably encountered countless viral food trends. Platforms like TikTok and Instagram can propel previously unknown foods to global fame. Everything from decadent Dubai chocolate to humble cottage cheese has enjoyed a moment in the spotlight, and entire businesses and careers now thrive on pure "Instagramability." Yet creating or discovering the next big food trend is far from simple, and these trends often carry real-world consequences.

According to Oxford psychologist Charles Spence, foods such as Dubai chocolate go viral because they appeal to multiple senses. Their contrasting colors make them visually satisfying, while their high-calorie content triggers our desire to eat them. Reaction videos, in which creators show exaggerated enthusiasm while tasting food, also shape how we think we should feel. In addition, many viral foods originate in faraway countries, giving them an exotic appeal that captures viewers' curiosity.

Content creators further boost engagement by using attention-grabbing titles. Phrases like "The World's TASTIEST Cake" or "The BEST EVER Pasta" make videos look too good to skip. Once a video receives enough views or comments, social media algorithms push it to more users' feeds, increasing its visibility and helping it go viral.

Although social media fuels these viral food trends, their effects often spill into the offline world. Some products experience supply shortages when interest suddenly skyrockets. For example, in 2021, Saxelby Cheesemongers, a cheese shop in New York, reported that it couldn't buy feta after a recipe for oven-baked feta pasta went viral. Likewise, restaurants and stores that unexpectedly gain popularity online often struggle to keep up with the surges in customers that result.

There are also potential health risks associated with online food trends. Many viral foods are high in fat or sugar, so eating too many could lead to health problems. Conversely, dieting-focused trends may encourage unhealthy eating habits or even contribute to eating disorders. Social comparison adds another layer of risk: When other people's lives appear exciting while your own seems dull, it can lead to low self-esteem and unrealistic expectations.

Online food trends can certainly be entertaining. They allow us to explore new flavors, discover creative recipes, and appreciate the wide variety of foods around the world. However, like all online phenomena, they should be enjoyed in moderation. It's worth remembering that the most meaningful parts of life happen away from our screens.
