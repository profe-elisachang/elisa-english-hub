🚦 Common Courtesy While Driving — Is it Really a Big Deal?

Peter and John use different forms of transportation to get to work. Peter likes to ride his scooter, especially when the weather is nice. But if the weather is cold or rainy, he takes the bus. John thinks driving his car to the office is convenient. That way he can get to work quickly, no matter what the weather is like.

📦 Vocabulary — Paragraph 1
transportation
(n) ways of getting from one place to another
transporte
"She uses public transportation to get to work."
scooter
(n) a small two-wheeled motor vehicle
scooter / motocicleta pequeña
"He rides a scooter to school every day."
convenient
(adj) easy to use; saves time or effort
conveniente / cómodo
"Living near the office is very convenient."

John: Hey, Peter. How was traffic this morning?
Peter: Traffic was really terrible today, John.
John: Why? Did something in particular happen?
Peter: The car in front of me wanted to turn left. But the driver didn't use his turn signal and stopped suddenly in the intersection. He almost caused an accident!
John: I'm glad there was no accident.
Peter: Me, too. People need to use some common courtesy when they drive.

📦 Vocabulary — Paragraph 2
turn signal
(n) the light on a car that shows you are turning
señal de vuelta / direccional
"Always use your turn signal before changing lanes."
intersection
(n) a place where two roads cross each other
intersección / cruce
"Stop at every intersection and check both ways."
common courtesy
(n) basic polite behavior that most people follow
cortesía básica / buenos modales
"It's common courtesy to hold the door for the next person."

John: What do you mean?
Peter: For starters, people should use their turn signals properly. That helps others know what you plan to do.
John: I don't know. Using turn signals doesn't seem like a big deal to me. Drivers can pay attention to what's happening on the road and respond accordingly.
Peter: Showing respect for other drivers doesn't take much effort. For example, allowing someone to merge in front of you can make a big difference during the morning commute.
John: Allowing other cars to cut in front of you slows you down.
Peter: Not really. Everyone is sharing the same road. So we need to be mindful of other vehicles.

John: You're one to talk! You ride a scooter. Scooters are always zooming past me and squeezing in between larger vehicles to pass them.
Peter: That can be dangerous, but it can also be dangerous for scooters to be stuck on the road between cars or buses. Tailgating is a serious problem.
John: Tailgating is only an issue if people are not paying attention while driving or have bad brakes.
Peter: I don't know about that. I feel nervous when a car is really close behind me. The driver could easily put some space between our vehicles.
John: If you're that worried about driving etiquette, maybe you should just take the bus more often.
Peter: Or maybe I should get a new job as a traffic officer! 🚔

🗣️ Discussion Questions
  • Q1. Do you think it is important to use turn signals while driving? Why or why not? Does everyone do this in your city?
  • Q2. Have you ever experienced bad driving behavior on your way to work? What happened and how did you feel?
  • Q3. Peter says, "Everyone is sharing the same road." Do you agree that drivers, scooters, and buses should all show more courtesy to each other? What could each group do better?
✏️ Self-Practice Questions
1. What happened to Peter on his way to work that morning? +
A car in front of Peter wanted to turn left, but the driver did not use his turn signal. The driver stopped suddenly in the intersection and almost caused an accident.
2. What does Peter think drivers should do to show "common courtesy"? +
Peter thinks drivers should use their turn signals properly so others know what they plan to do. He also thinks drivers should allow other vehicles to merge in front of them, especially during the morning commute. He believes everyone should be mindful of other vehicles on the road.
3. Why does John criticize scooter riders during their conversation? +
John points out that scooters are always zooming past cars and squeezing in between larger vehicles. He uses this to challenge Peter's argument about road courtesy — since Peter rides a scooter, John feels Peter is not in a position to complain about other drivers.
4. What does the idiom "You're one to talk" mean? Can you use it in a sentence from your work? +
"You're one to talk" means that the person criticizing others has the same problem or fault. It is used to point out that someone is a hypocrite.

Work example: "You're one to talk about being late — you missed the last two meetings!"
