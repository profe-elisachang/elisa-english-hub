🌊 Article: A New Wind-Powered Cargo Ship

A French company called Vela has designed a special cargo ship. It looks like a racing yacht, but it carries goods across the ocean. The ship runs on wind power — 100% of the time. It uses technology from the world of racing to move as fast as possible. The ship has three hulls, which help it cut through the water efficiently. The mast is made from carbon fiber, and the sails are made from special high-performance fabric.

Vela designed this ship to help companies decarbonize their transportation. Many companies ship pharmaceuticals, luxury cosmetics, and other high-end products by air. Air freight is fast, but it creates a lot of CO₂. It is often the biggest part of a company's carbon footprint. A regular diesel cargo ship is cheaper, but most of those ships are unreliable — they have long delays at ports.

🚢 Solving the Port Problem

Traditional container ships have become bigger and bigger. They are so large that they can only dock at a few main ports. This creates a bottleneck. Vela's ship is much smaller — only about 220 feet long, compared to 1,300 feet for a large container ship. Because it is smaller, it can use less-crowded ports and avoid long lines. It also loads and unloads much faster than a big container ship.

🌿 Clean, Quiet, and Fast

Vela's ship runs almost entirely on wind. It cuts emissions by 99% compared to air freight and by 90% compared to regular cargo ships. It also doesn't make noise as it sails — which is better for marine life like whales, which use sound to communicate. It doesn't use ballast water, so it doesn't pollute the ocean. The ship's body is made from aluminum — lighter than steel and easier to recycle.

💰 Good for Business Too

Because Vela's ship arrives faster and with fewer delays, it is more reliable for customers. For high-value cargo like pharmaceuticals, a single pallet can be worth up to $1 million. When cargo stays inside the ship until it reaches its destination, it is safer and better protected. Companies that care about sustainability can use this ship to reduce their carbon footprint without losing speed.

✏️ Self-Practice Questions
❓ How many hulls does Vela's ship have, and why is that important?
It has three hulls. This design (called a trimaran) helps the ship cut through water more efficiently and carry large sails — making it faster than a normal cargo ship.
❓ Why can Vela's ship use smaller ports? Give two reasons.
First, the ship is much smaller (220 feet vs. 1,300 feet for a large container ship). Second, because it is smaller, it can access less-crowded terminals and load/unload faster.
❓ In what two ways is the ship better for the environment compared to traditional ships?
1) It cuts emissions by 99% compared to air freight and 90% compared to regular cargo ships. 2) It doesn't create noise (protecting marine life) and doesn't use ballast water (no ocean pollution).
❓ Why is this ship especially useful for companies that ship pharmaceuticals?
Pharmaceutical cargo is high-value (a single pallet can be worth $1 million). The ship is more reliable (fewer delays), keeps cargo safe inside until delivery, and helps companies reduce their carbon footprint — which many clients require.
