📖 Article


🪙 Europe in Your Palm
National Pride or Pocket Burden?

Take a trip to Europe, and you're almost certain to handle some euro coins. No matter which country you are in, coins of the same denomination share consistent size and weight. However, every member state has its own series of coins featuring unique designs. Someone in Spain may end up with a coin representing Greece, a country at the opposite end of the continent.

On one side of every coin, the geographic outline of Europe is shown. On the other, a ring of 12 stars surrounds a design representing one country in the European Union (EU). In this way, every euro coin has two separate identities: one symbolizing the continent, and the other reflecting a national culture.

In addition to the €1 and €2 coins, there are coins worth 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, and 50 cents. When a new member state joins the union, they can design a series of coins that represents their culture. Many coins feature monarchs, such as those of Belgium, the Netherlands, and Spain. Some countries choose traditional symbols that represent their nation. For example, the German coins feature an eagle, while the Irish coins feature a Celtic harp.

For citizens of the EU, the euro coins are more than just currency. They are small but meaningful symbols of each nation that makes up the union. They are freely carried across national borders, recognized for their value, and accepted as payment in Eurozone countries, and even in some non-EU states like Andorra and Vatican City. These coins represent the hope that the community of European nations will remain united for many generations to come.

The weight and thickness of each euro coin vary according to its value. In general, heavier and thicker coins tend to have higher values, though there are exceptions. This system helps users distinguish between lower- and higher-value coins more easily. However, with eight different values of coins from 1 cent to €2, some people think the EU may have introduced too many coin types.

In recent years, with the rise of cashless payment methods, many people have begun to question the need for so many coins. Some European countries have effectively stopped using the smaller denomination coins in daily transactions. Supporters of phasing out the coins point out that minting the small coins often costs more than their actual face value. Others argue that carrying so many tiny coins is an inconvenience.

As of 2026, eight countries have officially implemented or widely adopted cash-rounding policies for the smallest euro coins. These nations ask businesses to round the total price up or down to the nearest 5 cents for all cash purchases. This way, people can largely avoid using the 1- and 2-cent coins. Currently, Belgium, Finland, Ireland, Italy, Slovakia, Estonia, Lithuania, and the Netherlands have effectively removed them from everyday circulation. However, other countries continue to mint these coins, which have not been abolished and remain legal tender throughout the Eurozone.

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

1. What do the two sides of a euro coin represent? ▼
One side of every euro coin shows the geographic outline of Europe surrounded by 12 stars, representing the European Union as a whole. The other side features a design chosen by each individual country, reflecting its own national culture and identity — for example, a monarch, an animal, or a traditional symbol.
2. Why do some people think the EU introduced too many coin types? ▼
The EU uses eight different coin values ranging from 1 cent to €2. Critics argue that this creates unnecessary complexity, making it harder to manage change quickly. In addition, producing the smallest coins — the 1- and 2-cent pieces — often costs more to mint than they are actually worth, which many people see as a waste of resources.
3. What does "cash rounding" mean, and how does it work? ▼
Cash rounding is a policy where the total price of a cash purchase is rounded up or down to the nearest 5 cents. This means customers and businesses avoid using 1- and 2-cent coins entirely. For example, a total of €2.83 would be rounded down to €2.80, while €2.87 would be rounded up to €2.90. Eight EU countries had adopted this system as of 2026.
4. How does the weight and thickness of euro coins help people? ▼
In general, higher-value euro coins are heavier and thicker, which allows users to distinguish between them by feel alone, without looking. This is especially helpful for visually impaired people or when reaching into a pocket or bag. Although there are some exceptions to this rule, the physical design is intentionally different for each denomination to make everyday transactions easier.
