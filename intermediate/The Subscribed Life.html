📊 The Subscribed Life

From Spotify to Netflix, the subscription economy is everywhere. Its scope extends far beyond what many of us think, though. Various subscription models are quietly reshaping the way we spend and manage our money.

• Access subscription model

Under this model, customers pay for the right to use a service or enjoy a range of benefits. While most people associate it with streaming platforms, it covers more than just digital entertainment. For example, the UK's Pret A Manger provides members with daily discounts on several drinks through a monthly subscription plan. While it offers users more for less, there is a catch for the businesses involved. They must provide consistent value and smart pricing to prevent members from leaving once the initial excitement fades.

• Replenishment subscription model

Replenishment subscriptions focus on convenience by delivering products that are used regularly on a fixed schedule. Whether it's pet food or soap, the system automates shopping so users never have to worry about restocking. This creates a win-win situation where people get essentials effortlessly, and businesses gain steady support. However, companies must carefully manage delivery frequency. If items begin to pile up at home, users often feel overwhelmed and cancel the service.

• Curation subscription model

This model appeals to those who enjoy the excitement of themed collections delivered straight to their doorstep. It allows subscribers to explore new brands without the need for extensive research. However, a lot of hard work goes on behind the scenes. Companies must manage complex shipping schedules and ensure that every package feels new and exciting. Without constant creativity, the magic of the surprise can quickly disappear, making it difficult to maintain long-term interest.

✏️ Self-Practice Questions
Q1 What is the main difference between the access model and the replenishment model? ▼
The access model gives customers the right to use a service (like Netflix or Spotify), while the replenishment model automatically delivers physical products that people need on a regular basis, like soap or pet food. The access model is more about enjoyment or digital benefits; the replenishment model is about saving time and never running out of essentials.
Q2 According to the article, what is the "catch" for businesses that use the access subscription model? ▼
The "catch" is that businesses must consistently provide real value and maintain smart pricing. If the service stops feeling worth the money — especially after the initial excitement fades — subscribers will cancel. This means companies can never stop working to improve the experience.
Q3 Why might a customer cancel a replenishment subscription, even if it was useful at first? ▼
A customer might cancel if deliveries come too often and products start to pile up at home. When people feel overwhelmed by having too much stock, or if the delivery frequency doesn't match how fast they actually use the product, they may decide to cancel the service to avoid waste and clutter.
Q4 In your own words, explain why creativity is so important for companies that use the curation model. ▼
The whole appeal of the curation model is the excitement of surprise. Customers pay to discover new products and brands. If the boxes start to feel repetitive or boring, the "magic" disappears and customers lose interest. Companies must constantly find fresh, interesting items to keep subscribers curious and engaged month after month.
