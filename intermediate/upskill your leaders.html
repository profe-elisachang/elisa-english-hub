📰 Today's Article: No, you can't upskill your culture. But you can upskill your leaders — Fast Company

Read Full Article ↗
💡 Article Takeaway
  • 🏗️ Culture is built by conversations, not posters. Values workshops and strategy days don't change behavior. Real culture comes from thousands of everyday interactions.
  • 😴 Most communication failures are unconscious. Leaders often repeat bad habits without noticing — just like the author who interrupted his manager, then did the same to his own team five minutes later.
  • 📉 Engagement is falling — and managers are the key. Global engagement dropped to 20% in 2024. Research shows 70% of team engagement depends on the direct manager.
  • 🦸 Three communication superpowers make the difference: Empathy (making people feel they matter), Clarity (giving clear direction people can remember), and Energy (showing genuine passion in how you speak and move).
  • ✅ Good news: these skills are trainable. Unlike "culture," communication is something leaders can actually practice and improve.
📖 Key Vocabulary
upskill
ES: mejorar habilidades / capacitar
To learn new skills or teach someone new skills, especially for work.
"The company wants to upskill its managers in communication."
unconscious
ES: inconsciente
Done without realizing it; not aware of your own actions or habits.
"She has an unconscious habit of interrupting people."
engagement
ES: compromiso / motivación laboral
How motivated and emotionally connected employees feel toward their work.
"High engagement means people work harder because they care."
slip back
ES: volver a caer / recaer
To return to a bad habit or behavior you wanted to stop.
"Even after training, it's easy to slip back into old habits."
Quiet Quitting
ES: renuncia silenciosa
When an employee does only the minimum required — no extra effort, no initiative.
"If the team feels ignored, they start quiet quitting."
turnover
ES: rotación de personal
The rate at which employees leave a company and need to be replaced.
"High turnover is expensive and hurts team morale."
ripple effect
ES: efecto dominó / onda expansiva
When one event causes a series of other events, like ripples in water.
"A manager's bad mood creates a ripple effect on the whole team."
empathy
ES: empatía
The ability to understand and share how another person feels.
"Good leaders show empathy by listening before they speak."
erode
ES: erosionar / desgastar
To slowly destroy or weaken something over time.
"Poor communication erodes trust between managers and teams."
comply
ES: cumplir / obedecer
To do what is required, often without enthusiasm or extra effort.
"They comply with the rules but don't go above and beyond."
attribute to
ES: atribuir a
To say that something is caused by or comes from a specific source.
"You can attribute most team problems to poor communication."
manifests
ES: se manifiesta / se expresa
Appears or shows itself in a visible way.
"His passion manifests in the way he speaks to the team."

✏️ Self-Practice — Think & Answer
❓ The article says "you can't upskill culture." What does that mean?
Culture is too big and abstract to change through a single workshop or training session. Culture is the result of thousands of small conversations and daily behaviors. You can't teach culture directly — but you can teach leaders to communicate better, and that changes behavior, which slowly changes culture.
❓ What is the difference between "complying" and being "engaged"?
When people comply, they do only what is required — nothing more. They follow the rules because they have to. When people are engaged, they work with energy and creativity because they want to. Engaged employees go beyond the minimum and take initiative.
❓ Why does the author say that "energy" is a communication skill?
Energy refers to your non-verbal communication — how you look, how you sound, your body language and tone. People read these signals before they even process the words. A leader who speaks in a flat, distracted way signals that the message is not important. Showing genuine enthusiasm tells your team: "This matters."
❓ The author says 70% of team engagement depends on the manager. Is that surprising? Why?
Many people think engagement depends on salary, company benefits, or the work itself. But Gallup's research shows that the direct manager has the biggest impact — bigger than almost anything else. This means that one person's daily communication habits can make or break an entire team's motivation and results.
