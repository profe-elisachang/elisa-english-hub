🕶️ The Return of Retro Aesthetics
— Eric Chen

You often hear about fashion being cyclical: trends come and go like waves crashing and retreating. While this phenomenon has been observed since the 1930s, the latest fashion wave among young people is different. It now encompasses just about anything with a retro aesthetic, including vintage technology.

One practical reason for the return of retro styles is that vintage clothing offers a sustainable alternative to fast fashion. However, for many people, going retro is really about standing out. In contrast to fast fashion, vintage clothing gives people more options for expressing their creativity. For example, you could pair '70s bell-bottom jeans with Y2K-style tiny sunglasses, an '80s leather jacket, and modern sneakers. Rather than replicating a single era exactly, people can create their own unique nostalgic looks.

Retro aesthetics are also trending in technology. Some young people are switching to older cameras such as CCD, film, or instant cameras. Compared to smartphones, these devices produce softer colors, grainier textures, and small imperfections that feel more romantic and authentic. CD players are another example — they're making a comeback both as an alternative to music streaming apps and as a fashion accessory. Together, these trends capture the spirit of a remix, blending old and new into distinctly personal styles.

🗣️ Discussion Questions
  • 1 Do you own anything "retro" — old technology, vintage clothing, or something from the past that you still use? Why do you keep it? Does it make you feel nostalgic?
  • 2 The article says young people like vintage styles because they want to stand out. In your workplace or social life, do you prefer to stand out — or to fit in? Is there pressure to dress or look a certain way?
  • 3 Fast fashion is cheap but not sustainable. Vintage clothing is more sustainable but can be harder to find. What do you think is more important when buying clothes — price, style, or sustainability? Has your thinking on this changed recently?
✍️ Self-Practice Questions

Click each question to reveal a model answer.

1 How would you explain the difference between "replicating" and "being inspired by" something? Give a work example. +
"Replicating" means copying something exactly — making it identical to the original. "Being inspired by" means using something as a starting point but adding your own ideas. At work, if I replicate a report, I just copy the format. But if I'm inspired by it, I might use a similar structure but change the data presentation to better fit our client's needs.
2 Describe something in your life or work that is "making a comeback" — an old idea, process, or tool that people are using again. +
In our purchasing department, we used to track everything in printed spreadsheets before switching to software. Interestingly, some team members are making a comeback with handwritten notes during meetings. They say it helps them focus better than typing. It's a simple tool, but it's coming back because it works.
3 Use "in contrast to" to compare two things in your industry or daily life. +
In contrast to our old inspection process, which required printing dozens of forms and filing them manually, our new digital system lets us record data directly on a tablet. The process is faster and errors are easier to catch. However, in contrast to what I expected, some experienced workers still prefer the paper forms because they're easier to read at a glance on the production floor.
4 Can you think of something "authentic" that your company or region is known for? How important is authenticity in your field? +
Our factory is known for producing authentic leather goods with traditional stitching techniques. In contrast to mass-produced items, our products have small handmade details that customers value. Authenticity is very important in our field — buyers are willing to pay more for something they know is genuinely handcrafted. We always make sure our marketing is honest about our process.
