Ten Ways to Improve Your Life

Small changes, big results — tips to help you get up and get the most out of life

Lifestyle A2–B1 Level By Barton Goldsmith


💬 Useful Expressions

get the most out of something
sacar el mayor provecho de algo
"We need to get the most out of every supplier visit — prepare your questions in advance."
on a regular basis
de forma regular / con frecuencia
"Our team reviews quality reports on a regular basis to catch defects early."
own up to something
admitir / reconocer un error
"When there's a shipment error, it's better to own up to it immediately than wait for the client to notice."
make a difference
hacer una diferencia / tener impacto
"Even small process improvements can really make a difference in our monthly output numbers."
tune into something
prestar atención a / sintonizar con
"A good team leader tunes into how their employees are feeling, especially during busy seasons."

🌿 Ten Ways to Improve Your Life

Do you ever feel like you aren't getting enough out of life? By doing a few simple things, you can brighten your days and make your life more fulfilling.

① Get outside more often

It has been said that if you want to live a long life, you should spend as much time outdoors as you can while doing things that are not too physically stressful. Taking a half-hour walk every day will help you avoid depression and decrease any anxiety you may be feeling.

fulfilling
satisfactorio / gratificante
Making you feel happy and satisfied because your work or life is meaningful.
"Working in QA is fulfilling because you know your work keeps the product safe."
anxiety
ansiedad / nerviosismo
A feeling of worry and nervousness, especially about something uncertain.
"Before a big audit, many workers feel anxiety about whether everything is in order."
decrease
disminuir / reducir
To become or make something smaller in number, amount, or size.
"Our goal this quarter is to decrease the number of rejected parts by 15%."

② Trust your gut

Tune into your feelings, look at them, and work on understanding where the pain is coming from. All of the answers you need are there inside you.

tune into
sintonizar con / prestar atención a
(Phrasal verb) To pay close attention to your own feelings or to a situation around you.
"Good supervisors tune into team morale and know when people need a break."
trust your gut
confiar en tu instinto
(Idiom) To follow your instinct or inner feeling, even without clear evidence.
"If the supplier's numbers don't feel right, trust your gut and ask for more documentation."

③ Make a new friend

You may think you have enough friends already or that you don't have time to make new friends, but there is always room to add a positive person into your life. The idea is to get closer to people you may already know or see on a regular basis.

on a regular basis
de forma regular / con frecuencia
Repeatedly, at regular times — as part of a routine or habit.
"We share production updates with the client on a regular basis — usually every Monday."
get closer to
acercarte a (alguien) / fortalecer una relación
(Phrasal verb) To develop a stronger relationship or better understanding with someone.
"Team lunches are a good way to get closer to colleagues from other departments."

④ Get involved in your community

It's hard to open a paper and not see the charity events going on close by. If you have the desire to help out, act on it. You will make a difference in the lives of others, and you will feel better, too.

charity
caridad / organización benéfica
Giving help — money, time, or goods — to people who need it, often through organizations.
"Some companies donate part of their profits to charity as part of their social responsibility."
act on
actuar en base a / tomar acción
(Phrasal verb) To do something in response to information, a feeling, or a plan.
"Once the logistics team spotted the delay risk, they acted on it immediately."
make a difference
hacer una diferencia / tener impacto
(Idiom) To have a positive and noticeable effect on something or someone.
"Consistent feedback from the QA team really makes a difference in final product quality."

⑤ Slow down

We rush so much of the time, and it's understandable with all that's going on around us. But if you relax for a moment here and there, just to take in your surroundings and what you are doing, it will make you breathe easier and help you enjoy whatever you are doing more.

surroundings
entorno / ambiente / lo que te rodea
The things and conditions around you in a particular place or situation.
"A clean and organized workspace improves your surroundings and helps you focus."
breathe easier
sentirte con menos presión / relajarte
(Idiom) To feel less worried or under pressure; to relax after a stressful period.
"Once the customs clearance was approved, the whole logistics team could finally breathe easier."

⑥ Appreciate what you have

We all want things, but when not getting what you want makes you sad and angry, you may be asking for too much too soon. Look around at your life and focus on the good stuff, see what you have, and don't focus on what you lack.

lack
carecer de / falta de
To not have something, or to not have enough of it.
"The new employee doesn't lack motivation — she just needs more technical training."
appreciate
valorar / apreciar
To recognize the value or importance of something and feel grateful for it.
"Clients really appreciate it when we deliver ahead of schedule."

⑦ Try a new activity

Take a class, go some place you've never been, or create a new space in your home. Taking on hobbies and activities can be enjoyable, and it gives you the opportunity to meet new people and experience new things that will enrich your life.

enrich
enriquecer / mejorar la calidad
To improve the quality or value of something by adding something extra.
"Cross-training in different departments can enrich your skills and make you more valuable."
opportunity
oportunidad
A time or situation that makes it possible to do something you want to do.
"The trade show is a great opportunity to meet new suppliers and compare prices."

⑧ Write your story

Many people are tracing their family histories online these days. You can also research and write your own story. It will give you a fresh perspective on your life and allow you to remember things that you may have forgotten. If writing an autobiography is too daunting, try keeping a journal. Then your story can write itself.

perspective
perspectiva / punto de vista
A particular way of thinking about something, especially one that is influenced by your experience or knowledge.
"Seeing the problem from the customer's perspective helps us design better solutions."
daunting
intimidante / abrumador
Seeming very difficult or frightening, making you feel less confident about doing it.
"Managing a new international account can feel daunting at first, but it gets easier."

⑨ Make that apology

If there is someone in your life to whom you need to say, "I'm sorry," go ahead and do it. Letting go of resentments and owning up to your mistakes will give you more room to enjoy your time on the planet. It may also help you connect with a long-lost friend.

resentment
resentimiento / rencor
A bitter feeling of anger toward someone because of unfair treatment.
"Resentment between departments grows when credit for results is not shared fairly."
own up to
admitir / reconocer un error
(Phrasal verb) To admit that you have done something wrong or made a mistake.
"It's better to own up to a shipping error right away than to wait for the client to find out."
long-lost
perdido hace mucho tiempo
Lost or separated from someone for a long period of time.
"Reconnecting with a long-lost colleague can sometimes open unexpected business doors."

⑩ Plan some fun

Happiness comes from moving toward what you want, not from getting it, so you need to have new things to look forward to. Now is a good time to start planning for next year or your next vacation.

If you are just sitting around and letting the world pass you by, it's time to get up and start moving. Getting the most out of life is a matter of putting one foot in front of the other and making it happen.

pass you by
dejarte atrás / perder una oportunidad
(Phrasal verb) If something passes you by, you miss it or don't take advantage of it.
"Don't let the chance to apply for that supervisory role pass you by — submit your application today."
put one foot in front of the other
avanzar paso a paso / seguir adelante
(Idiom) To continue making steady progress, especially when things are difficult.
"When the project timeline is tight, just keep putting one foot in front of the other and you'll get there."
look forward to
tener algo que esperar / estar emocionado por
(Phrasal verb) To feel excited and pleased about something that is going to happen.
"Having a team event planned gives everyone something to look forward to during a long production run."

✏️ Self-Practice Questions

1. According to the article, what are two benefits of spending time outdoors?
▶ Show Answer
Spending time outdoors helps you avoid depression and decrease anxiety. The article recommends a half-hour walk every day as a simple way to enjoy these benefits.
2. What does the article suggest if writing a full autobiography feels too daunting?
▶ Show Answer
The article suggests keeping a journal as a simpler alternative. Over time, it says, "your story can write itself."
3. What does "own up to your mistakes" mean? Use it in a sentence about your job.
▶ Show Answer
Own up to (phrasal verb) = to admit that you did something wrong.

Example: "When I sent the wrong part number to the supplier, I decided to own up to it right away and call them to correct the order."
4. In your own words, what does "putting one foot in front of the other" mean? When might you use this expression at work?
▶ Show Answer
This idiom means to keep making progress step by step, especially when something is difficult or overwhelming.

Example at work: "During peak season with a huge backlog of orders, you just have to keep putting one foot in front of the other and process them one by one."
