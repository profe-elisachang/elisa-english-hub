Connection, Not Control

A pediatrician's advice on talking to teens about drugs and alcohol — and why trust works better than strict rules, at home and at work.

Level: A2–B1 Topic: Family & Communication Skill: Listening for advice language

🩺 Article: A Pediatrician's Advice on Talking to Teens

key vocabulary phrasal verb idiom

1️⃣ Connection Matters More Than Control

I have talked with many teens and parents about drugs and alcohol. I learned one important thing: Connection matters more than control. Parents often say to me, "I'm scared my teen might be using drugs. But I don't know how to start the conversation." These fears are normal.

Most teens do not use drugs or alcohol. But some do. Adolescence is a time to explore and take risks. Today's drugs are more dangerous than before. For example, there is high-potency cannabis. There are also counterfeit pills. Some weed is laced with fentanyl. But here is good news: you have more influence than you think.

When parents worry, they often want to tighten the reins. They take away the teen's phone. They search the teen's room. They threaten punishment. But research shows something different: teens need connection, not control. Instead of punishment, start a conversation.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 1

Connection
the relationship or bond between people
la conexión, el vínculo
Building a strong connection with your team works better than strict supervision.
Adolescence
the period of life between childhood and adulthood (roughly 12–18)
la adolescencia
During adolescence, teens often seek independence and new experiences.
High-potency
very strong or powerful, especially in effect on the body
de alta potencia, muy concentrado
The new cleaning solvent is high-potency, so operators must use gloves.
Counterfeit
made to look like the real thing, in order to deceive
falsificado, imitación
The purchasing team rejected the shipment after finding counterfeit parts.
📌 Compare: counterfeit = made to deceive (illegal). Fake = not real (broader, not always illegal).
Laced
mixed with a substance (often something dangerous)
mezclado con (frecuentemente algo peligroso)
The report was laced with errors, so it went back to QA for review.
⚠️ Always used with "with" — laced with [something]. Never used alone.
Tighten the reins
to increase control or restrict someone's freedom
aumentar el control, ser más estricto
After the incident, the plant manager decided to tighten the reins on overtime approvals.

2️⃣ Start the Conversation Early

Ask open questions. For example: "What have you heard about vaping at school?" or "How do your friends feel about drinking?" When teens feel judged, they stop talking. When teens feel heard, they open up. Trust protects them. But scare tactics can backfire. They can hurt communication. Then teens do not come to you when they have a problem.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 2

Vaping
inhaling vapor from electronic cigarettes or similar devices
vapeo, uso de cigarrillos electrónicos
Vaping has become a health and safety topic in many break rooms.
Scare tactics
methods that use fear to persuade someone to do or not do something
tácticas de miedo
Scare tactics with suppliers often fail because they see through them.
Backfire
to have the opposite result from what was intended
tener el efecto contrario, salir mal
His attempt to speed up the line backfired and created more defects.

3️⃣ What's Changed Since Last Generation

Substance use looks different now. Alcohol is still the most common substance for teens. But cannabis use is also common now, especially vapes and edibles. Nicotine vaping has grown a lot in recent years. Learn about what teens really use. This helps you have better conversations.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 3

Edibles
food items that contain cannabis or other drugs
comestibles (con cannabis)
Edibles can be misleading because the effects take longer to appear.
📌 Note: in the US, "edibles" usually means food with cannabis. In other contexts it can simply mean "food."
Nicotine
a highly addictive chemical substance found in tobacco
la nicotina
Nicotine addiction is hard to break, even for experienced smokers.

4️⃣ When to Get Professional Help

Sometimes it is hard to know if a teen is experimenting or really struggling. Watch for changes in mood, sleep, appetite, school grades, or friendships. If you see these changes, talk to your pediatrician. Pediatricians know how to check for substance use and mental health problems. Also, teens often feel more comfortable talking to a doctor than to a parent.

One important goal is to delay substance use for as long as possible. If a teen starts using substances early, they have a higher risk of a substance use disorder later. The goal is not perfection. The goal is protection. Even if your teen has already tried something, you can still help.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 4

Pediatrician
a doctor who specializes in treating children and adolescents
el pediatra
Our pediatrician gave us helpful advice on talking to our teen.
Disorder
a medical condition or illness that disrupts normal function
el trastorno, la enfermedad
Substance use disorder is a treatable medical condition, not a personal failure.

5️⃣ Practical Strategies for Talking

Be curious. Ask questions like, "What would you do if someone offered you a drink at a party?" Play to your teen's strengths. For example, maybe your teen has an independent streak. Tap into that. You might say, "You never follow the crowd. How can that help you make good choices if friends use drugs or alcohol?"

Your relationship with your teen is the most powerful tool you have. Don't worry about one perfect speech. Small conversations over time matter more. Talk during a car ride. Talk while washing dishes. Talk at Sunday breakfast or at bedtime. Be curious. Be calm. Let your teen know you are always there to talk.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 5

Play to someone's strengths
to emphasize or use a person's positive qualities
aprovechar las fortalezas de alguien
Good supervisors play to their team's strengths instead of focusing on weaknesses.
⚠️ Don't confuse with "play up to someone" (= to flatter someone for favor). They are opposite in meaning.
Streak
a distinct quality or tendency in someone's character
el rasgo, la tendencia (de personalidad)
She has a competitive streak that pushes her to meet every deadline.
📌 "Streak" can also mean a series (a 3-day streak). Here it means a personality tendency — a more formal synonym is "tendency."
Tap into
to get something useful out of something that already exists but hasn't been used yet
aprovechar, acceder a un recurso
The coach knew how to tap into the team's energy and motivation.
💡 Picture tapping a tree for sap — getting something useful out of something already there.

6️⃣ Playing the Long Game

TV and social media often show alcohol and drugs in a positive way. Sometimes you are the only person who says these things are not good. Tell your teen clearly: you don't want them to use drugs or alcohol. You might say, "I care about your health. I don't want you using alcohol or drugs while your brain is still growing." Your teen may roll their eyes. But research shows they still hear you.

📚 Vocabulary — Section 6

Roll their eyes
to show annoyance or disbelief by turning the eyes upward
poner los ojos en blanco (mostrar fastidio)
The new intern rolled his eyes when told to redo the safety checklist — but he did it anyway.
