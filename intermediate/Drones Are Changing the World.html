🚁 Drones Are Changing the World

And China Is Leading the Way — A B1 Reading Lesson

💬 Section 1: Useful Expressions

scale up
ampliar; escalar
The government helped factories scale up drone production across the country.
keep up (with)
mantenerse al día; seguir el ritmo
American companies are struggling to keep up with China's fast growth in drones.
wipe out
eliminar; acabar con
Low prices from Chinese companies wiped out many Western competitors.
attribute (something) to
atribuir algo a
Experts attribute DJI's success to its low prices and ease of use.
put (something) to work
poner algo en práctica; hacer que algo funcione
China is putting AI to work in real factories and delivery systems — not just in theory.

📖 Section 2 & 3: Article with Vocabulary

Paragraph 1 — Introduction

For many years, drones were only for the military or for hobbyists who flew them for fun. Today, drones are everywhere in China. They deliver food to pickup points, carry blood to hospitals, spray crops on farms, and help in emergencies. In 2024, China had 2.2 million civilian drones — five times more than just five years ago.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 1

hobbyist

A person who does an activity for fun, not for work or money

aficionado; entusiasta

Many hobbyists fly small drones on weekends for photography.
deliver

To bring or transport something to a person or place

entregar; llevar

The drone can deliver packages in under 30 minutes.
spray

To send liquid in small drops onto a surface

rociar; aplicar con spray

Agricultural drones spray fertilizer over large fields in minutes.
civilian

Not related to the military; for ordinary people

civil; no militar

The government supports civilian use of drones for business purposes.
Paragraph 2 — China's Strategy

This didn't happen by accident. The Chinese government made a deliberate plan to grow what it calls the "low-altitude economy." It changed its airspace rules and helped local governments build more drone factories. Today, China makes at least 70% of the world's commercial drones. Flying taxis — called eVTOL aircraft — may start carrying passengers as soon as next year.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 2

accident

Something that happens without being planned

accidente; casualidad

China's drone success was not an accident — it was carefully planned.
airspace

The sky above a country that is controlled by its government

espacio aéreo

New airspace rules allow drones to fly over cities more freely.
commercial

Related to business and making money

comercial

China makes 70% of the world's commercial drones.
passenger

A person who travels in a vehicle but does not drive it

pasajero

Flying taxis could carry passengers across the city by next year.
Paragraph 3 — The U.S. Is Falling Behind

The United States invented modern aircraft and built the first military drones. But today, American commercial drone services are still in early testing. Regulations are strict — pilots must keep drones in their line of sight. Only 53% of Americans said they would use drone delivery, compared to 86% in China.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 3

invent

To create or design something that did not exist before

inventar; crear

The Wright brothers invented the modern airplane in 1903.
regulation

An official rule made by a government or organization

regulación; regla oficial

Strict regulations make it harder to operate drones in the U.S.
line of sight

The direct path between your eyes and something you can see

línea de visión; lo que puedes ver directamente

U.S. rules require drones to stay within the pilot's line of sight.
strict

Very controlled; not allowing much freedom

estricto; riguroso

The factory has strict quality control rules for every product.
Paragraph 4 — One Company Rules the Market

A Chinese company called DJI controls about 80% of the U.S. commercial drone market. Why? Its drones are easy to use and less expensive than competitors. Some experts say DJI used government support and low prices to push Western companies out of the market. Because of security concerns, the U.S. Congress is now considering a ban on DJI products.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 4

competitor

A company or person competing against others in the same market

competidor; rival

DJI pushed many Western competitors out of the drone market.
security concern

A worry or risk related to safety or national security

preocupación de seguridad

Security concerns about data led Congress to review DJI's products.
ban

An official rule that stops something from being done or sold

prohibición; veto

The government is considering a ban on Chinese drone imports.
Congress

The national law-making body of the United States

Congreso (de EE.UU.)

Congress voted to review security risks from Chinese technology companies.
Paragraph 5 — Practice Makes Perfect

One expert compares China's advantage to cooking. You can have a great recipe and a good kitchen, but if you've never cooked before, you still won't know how to fry an egg. China has spent years building real hands-on experience — workers, factories, and companies all learning together. The city of Shenzhen is a perfect example: it became a global hub for hardware, where drone makers can easily find parts like batteries, cameras, and sensors.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 5

hands-on experience

Knowledge gained by actually doing something, not just studying it

experiencia práctica; aprender haciendo

Working on the assembly line gives you real hands-on experience.
hub

A central place where a lot of activity happens

centro; núcleo

Monterrey is becoming a major manufacturing hub in Mexico.
sensor

A device that detects or measures things like light, temperature, or distance

sensor

The drone uses sensors to avoid obstacles during flight.
hardware

Physical technology components and electronic equipment

hardware; componentes físicos

Shenzhen supplies hardware to tech companies around the world.
Paragraph 6 — The Bigger Picture

The drone story is part of a bigger difference between the U.S. and China. The U.S. is focused on developing very advanced AI systems that are still mostly theoretical. China is using AI in the real world — in factories, robots, and drones. In the race to apply new technology, China is moving fast.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraph 6

theoretical

Based on ideas and concepts, not real-world practice

teórico; conceptual

The U.S. is investing in AI that is still mostly theoretical.
apply

To use something in a practical situation

aplicar; usar en práctica

China is applying AI technology to real factories and logistics.
automate

To make a process work automatically without human control

automatizar

Chinese factories are automating more processes using robots and AI.
race

A competition to be the first or best at something

carrera; competencia

The U.S. and China are in a race to lead global technology.

🗣️ Section 5: Discussion Questions

  • In your job — in QA, logistics, purchasing, or sales — can you think of one task that a drone could help with? How would it change your work?
  • China changed its rules to allow more drone use. Do you think Mexico should do the same? What are the risks?
  • The expert says China is good at "learning by doing." Can you think of a skill you have at work that you learned by doing — not from a book or classroom?

✏️ Section 6: Self-Practice

1. Fill in the blank: China made a __________ plan — it was not an accident — to grow its drone industry.

(deliberate / civilian / commercial)

2. True or False: Most Americans said they are happy to use drone delivery services.

3. Match the word to the meaning:

a. hub    b. ban    c. regulation

1. An official rule made by a government    2. An official decision to stop something    3. A central place where a lot of activity happens

4. In your own words: Why does the expert compare China's advantage to cooking? Do you think it is a good comparison?

✅ Reference Answers — Click to reveal
  1. deliberate — China made a deliberate, planned decision to grow its drone industry through policy changes and government support.
  2. False — Only 53% of Americans said they would use drone delivery, compared to 86% in China.
  3. a → 3 (A central place where a lot of activity happens)  |  b → 2 (An official decision to stop something)  |  c → 1 (An official rule made by a government)
  4. The comparison works because knowing a recipe (having information) is not enough — you need real cooking experience to be good. In the same way, China's advantage in drones comes not just from knowledge, but from years of actually building, testing, and improving them.
