🛡️ The Importance of Personal Boundaries

Setting limits that protect your well-being and respect others

💬 Useful Expressions
to set a boundary
establecer un límite
My supervisor set a boundary about answering emails after 7 p.m.
to feel uncomfortable with something
sentirse incómodo/a con algo
I feel uncomfortable with last-minute requests that change my whole plan.
to put pressure on someone
presionar a alguien
The client kept putting pressure on the team to deliver early.
to wear down trust
desgastar la confianza
Ignoring agreements slowly wears down trust between colleagues.
a two-way street
una calle de doble sentido / algo recíproco
Good communication is a two-way street — both sides must listen.
📖 Article

There are all sorts of boundaries in the world around us, from walls and fences to rivers and mountains. These barriers help us keep things we want in and things we don't want out. However, not all boundaries are physical. Personal boundaries are rules or limits we set for ourselves in daily life. They range from professional restrictions to emotional guardrails and are essential because they help us decide what is or isn't good for us.

📘 Paragraph 1 — Key Words
boundary
noun
A line or limit that separates two things
🇲🇽 límite, frontera
"There is a clear boundary between work time and personal time."
barrier
noun
Something that blocks or separates
🇲🇽 barrera, obstáculo
"A language barrier can make meetings more difficult."
restriction
noun
A rule that limits what you can do
🇲🇽 restricción
"There are weight restrictions for packages in shipping."
guardrail
noun
A protection that prevents you from going too far in the wrong direction
🇲🇽 protección, guarda
"Good company policies act as guardrails for employees."
essential
adjective
Absolutely necessary; very important
🇲🇽 esencial, fundamental
"Clear deadlines are essential in logistics."

Peer pressure affects almost everyone, especially young people. If everyone around you is doing something, chances are you'll find it hard not to join them. This is why personal boundaries with friends are so important. If you feel uncomfortable in certain situations, be honest with your friends so they'll understand not to push you into activities you don't enjoy. Just as we set our own boundaries, we must respect others' boundaries too. Pay attention to verbal and nonverbal cues. If someone says "no" or seems uncomfortable, don't pressure them. Violating boundaries — whether by constantly texting late at night, sharing private information, or pushing unwanted activities — wears down trust and damages relationships. Respecting boundaries is a two-way street that creates healthier connections.

📘 Paragraph 2 — Key Words
peer pressure
noun phrase
The influence from people your own age or group to do something
🇲🇽 presión de grupo
"Peer pressure made him agree to work overtime every weekend."
cue
noun
A sign or signal that tells you something
🇲🇽 señal, indicio
"When the customer goes quiet, that's a cue they're unhappy."
nonverbal
adjective
Communication without words — body language, facial expressions
🇲🇽 no verbal
"Crossing your arms is a nonverbal sign that you are not comfortable."
violate
verb
To break a rule or go against someone's limits
🇲🇽 violar, infringir
"Sharing a colleague's personal data violates their privacy."
🔗 Phrasal Verb: wear down
wear down = to slowly reduce or destroy something, especially trust or energy  |  desgastar poco a poco
"Constant interruptions wear down a team's motivation."

Personal boundaries are also essential for managing technology in our lives. With modern devices, work can easily follow you home, but you can protect your personal time by turning off your work phone or putting off emails after a certain time. Social media poses additional challenges. Apps like Instagram and Facebook let us connect globally, yet they're so addictive they can consume too much of our time. To address this, set strict time limits for social media use, leaving time to enjoy the real world. Content restrictions can also filter out harmful material online.

📘 Paragraph 3 — Key Words
device
noun
A machine or tool, especially an electronic one
🇲🇽 dispositivo, aparato
"Our QA team uses devices to scan barcodes on the line."
addictive
adjective
Something that makes you want more and is hard to stop
🇲🇽 adictivo/a
"Checking notifications every five minutes is very addictive."
consume
verb
To use up a large amount of something (time, energy, resources)
🇲🇽 consumir, absorber
"Manual data entry consumes hours that could go to real analysis."
strict
adjective
Firm and exact; not flexible
🇲🇽 estricto/a, riguroso/a
"We have strict rules about safety equipment on the factory floor."
🔗 Phrasal Verbs in this paragraph:

put off = to delay or postpone  |  posponer, aplazar
"She put off answering the email until the next morning."

filter out = to remove something unwanted  |  filtrar, eliminar
"The system filters out suppliers who don't meet our standards."

Setting boundaries doesn't mean distancing yourself from others. Rather, boundaries provide a healthy way to navigate the world. They allow us to interact with others without neglecting our own needs. They're beneficial for both you and those around you.

🗣️ Discussion Questions

Talk about it with your classmates:

  1. Do you think it is easy or difficult to say "no" at work in Mexico? What makes it hard? Have you ever felt peer pressure from a manager or coworker?
  2. Technology makes it hard to "disconnect" from work. Do you check work messages after hours? What boundaries, if any, do you try to set?
  3. The article says respecting boundaries is "a two-way street." Can you think of a situation at work where one person's boundary affected the whole team?
✏️ Self-Practice
1. What does "peer pressure" mean? Give an example from your workplace.
Peer pressure means the influence from people around you (colleagues, friends) that pushes you to do something — even if you don't really want to.

Example: "Everyone on the team was staying late, so I felt peer pressure to stay too, even though I had already finished my work."
2. Choose the correct word: "Ignoring someone's 'no' slowly _______ trust." (wears down / filters out / puts off)
✅ wears down

"Ignoring someone's 'no' slowly wears down trust."

Remember: wear down = to slowly destroy or reduce. Filter out = to remove unwanted things. Put off = to delay.
3. Make your own sentence: "I feel uncomfortable when _______ at work."
There is no single right answer! Here are some ideas:

"I feel uncomfortable when my supervisor calls me after 9 p.m."
"I feel uncomfortable when someone shares my mistakes in front of the whole team."
"I feel uncomfortable when I am asked to skip my lunch break."
4. True or False: "Setting boundaries means you don't want to talk to anyone."
❌ False.

The article clearly says: "Setting boundaries doesn't mean distancing yourself from others." Boundaries help you interact with people in a healthier way — you still connect, but on terms that work for you.
