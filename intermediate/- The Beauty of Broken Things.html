🏺 The Beauty of Broken Things

Plates, bowls, and cups are fragile things. No matter how careful we are, they sometimes break. When that happens, most people just throw the broken items away—not everyone, though. In Japan, when something breaks, it is often fixed using the beautiful art of kintsugi.

📚 Key Vocabulary - Paragraph 1

fragile
easily broken or damaged
frágil
"Handle the package carefully—the contents are fragile."
careful
paying attention to avoid danger or mistakes
cuidadoso
"Be careful when operating the machinery."
throw away
to discard or get rid of something
tirar, desechar
"Don't throw away those materials—we can recycle them."

Kintsugi is the practice of repairing broken ceramics using a special glue mixed with gold powder. Instead of hiding the cracks, kintsugi highlights them. The golden lines create a unique and beautiful pattern, showing where the object once broke. Its history becomes part of its beauty.

📚 Key Vocabulary - Paragraph 2

objects made from clay and hardened by heat (pottery)

"Traditional ceramics are popular souvenirs in many Asian countries."

"Check the walls for cracks before painting."
highlights
to emphasize or make something more noticeable
resalta, destaca, enfatiza
"This report highlights the main quality issues we found."

As well as being a beautiful artistic practice, kintsugi has an important lesson to teach us about life. In the same way that objects become damaged or broken, people also go through difficult times and suffer hurt. However, instead of hiding our emotional or physical scars, kintsugi encourages us to embrace them. Our scars show that we have survived and grown stronger.

So, the next time you break a cup or bowl, consider giving it new life with kintsugi. Moreover, when you face challenges in your own life, remember: Your cracks don't make you weaker—they make you more beautiful!

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

+ What is kintsugi and how does it work?
Kintsugi is a Japanese art form that repairs broken ceramics using a special glue mixed with gold powder. Instead of hiding the cracks, kintsugi highlights them with golden lines. This creates a unique and beautiful pattern that shows where the object was broken, making the item's history part of its beauty. The philosophy behind kintsugi is that brokenness and repair are part of an object's story, not something to hide.
+ What life lesson does kintsugi teach us?
Kintsugi teaches us that we should embrace our emotional and physical scars instead of hiding them. Just like broken objects are repaired and made beautiful with gold, people who go through difficult times and experience pain can become stronger and more beautiful because of their experiences. Our "cracks" or challenges don't make us weaker—they show that we have survived and grown. This is a powerful metaphor for resilience and accepting imperfection.
+ Practice using "go through": Describe a challenge you or your team went through and how you handled it.
Example answer (create your own!):
"Last year, our team went through a very stressful period when we had to meet a tight deadline for an important client. We were working long hours and everyone was exhausted. However, instead of giving up, we supported each other and reorganized our workflow. In the end, we delivered on time and the client was very satisfied. Going through that difficult experience made our team stronger and more confident."
+ Compare: What would most people do with a broken cup vs. what does kintsugi teach us to do?
Most people: When something breaks, most people just throw it away and buy a new one. They see the broken item as useless or worthless.

Kintsugi approach: Kintsugi teaches us to repair the broken item and highlight the cracks with gold, making them part of the object's beauty and story. It shows us that broken things can become even more valuable and beautiful after being repaired.

Life application: Similarly, when we face failures or difficult times in life, we shouldn't try to hide them or pretend they didn't happen. Instead, we should embrace them as part of our journey that made us stronger and more interesting people.
