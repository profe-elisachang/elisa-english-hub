Part-Time Expertise
Paying a fraction of the cost for highly skilled workers

A new category of solopreneur is challenging perceptions of what it means to be self-employed. Since 2018, demand for fractional expertise — specialized talent that works for one or several companies on a limited, part-time basis — has tripled, according to a recent study by a workforce intelligence company. The most popular fractional executive role is CFO, making up 18% of all fractional positions, followed by CMO at 14% and CEO at 10%.

The study's chief economist explains that recent layoffs have freed up many highly skilled workers, while budgetary constraints have added to the appeal of a lower-cost, flexible alternative to full-time hires. "Employers see fractional work as a way to cut costs while still filling a role, and because the job market is weak, more people are willing to take these part-time positions," she says.

Separating Activities

"What I'm seeing is the unbundling of roles," explains one global CEO. "Roles we historically thought of as full-time and permanent are being broken down into smaller tasks, and the fractional trend meets that head on." Before COVID, she says, most people didn't understand fractional work when she pitched it to them — but the pandemic showed everyone that business could operate differently, and it normalized this model.

Control and Flexibility

The pandemic also inspired workers to reevaluate their relationship with work and look for more control over their schedules. It made many people consider whether a full-time, high-pressure leadership job was detrimental to their health, wellbeing, and family life. Prior to the pandemic, the main customers for fractional services were startups and small businesses that couldn't afford full-time help. That segment is still strong, but there has also been significant growth among larger organizations looking for temporary, flexible support.

Direct Access to Experts

"Since COVID, the pace of change — geopolitics, climate change, war, technology — has meant organizations have a lot to cope with, and senior leaders have started looking outside for help," one expert says. "They might have a CFO or CMO employed full-time, but those people are overwhelmed in a turbulent world, and they need a more agile talent structure."

When large organizations needed outside assistance in the past, they traditionally turned to major consulting firms. Many are now discovering they can access specialized talent at a lower cost by engaging a part-time expert — someone who may even have worked at one of those same consulting firms before.

Shifting Perception

Before the term "fractional" gained traction, people who offered this kind of service were given labels that didn't really match their level of expertise — consultant, contractor, 1099. "'Fractional' is a business term," one founder explains. "It says, 'I'm going to solve your problem, and I can do it in a fractional capacity.'"

That new label has shifted the perception of this category of professionals from something many felt they had to hide on their résumés to something they now advertise. "They say out loud, 'I'm so good at what I do that I can help your business in 10 or 20 hours a week,'" says Ran Harpaz, founder of a fintech platform for solopreneurs. "It's not a gig — it's a destination."
