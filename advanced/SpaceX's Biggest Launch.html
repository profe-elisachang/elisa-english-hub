🚀 SpaceX's Biggest Launch?

The prospect of buying stock in Elon Musk's aerospace company

by Laurence Darmiento / © 2025, Los Angeles Times. Distributed by Tribune Content Agency, LLC.

SpaceX has transformed the aerospace industry with its reusable rockets—radically lowering the cost of lifting satellites into space while bringing internet service to all corners of the globe with its Starlink network.

Now, Elon Musk's privately held company is eyeing what could be the largest initial public offering (IPO) ever as it faces multiple challenges—including landing Americans on the moon, satisfying the billionaire's ambitious if quixotic desires to colonize Mars and potentially putting AI data centers into orbit.

The company is seeking to raise more than $30 billion with an IPO that would value the company at $1.5 trillion, according to reports. The funding could speed development of its deep-space Starship spacecraft, key to its moon and Mars missions, as it explores the orbiting data center concept—all of which has drawn skepticism, given their need for cutting-edge technological advances.

❓ SpaceX has been private since it was founded in 2002. Why is it going public now?

Plain and simple, the company has a voracious appetite for capital. Musk said on his social media network X that the company's revenue would top $15 billion [in 2025], but when your goals are to land astronauts on the moon, colonize Mars and assist development of the most sophisticated AI, there is never enough money.

❓ Why put data centers in space?

Musk's artificial intelligence company xAI and its Grok chatbot are in a race against Google, OpenAI and other competitors to build the most sophisticated AI agents, leading to hundreds of billions of dollars of capital investments. But hampering all competitors are the huge power needs of data centers that perform all of the computations.

One so-called hyperscale facility has power needs equivalent to 100,000 households, according to the Pew Research Center. Putting data centers into Earth's orbit powered by a large solar cell array would give the chips 24-hour access to the sun's energy if the satellite is in what is called sun-synchronous orbit.

"Weather satellites often use a sun-synchronous orbit."

Musk has confirmed that data centers were a key reason for the IPO. He also could foresee launching data centers each year with aggregate 100-gigawatt power consumption—equivalent to more than 20% of the country's annual power consumption.

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

1. What does "a voracious appetite for capital" mean in this context?

It means the company needs an extremely large, ongoing amount of money to fund its ambitious goals, like landing astronauts on the moon and colonizing Mars.

2. Why does Musk want to put data centers in orbit?

Because data centers use enormous amounts of power, and a solar-powered satellite in sun-synchronous orbit would have constant, 24-hour access to solar energy.

3. What did astrophysicist Ehud Behar say about the feasibility of orbiting data centers?

He said there are serious technological hurdles, including how to cool the satellites, the risk that fast-advancing AI chips could make them obsolete, and the difficulty of repairing a malfunctioning satellite in space.

4. How does SpaceX's expected IPO valuation compare to Nvidia's market value?

A public offering could nearly double SpaceX's private valuation of about $800 billion, while Nvidia — currently the world's most valuable public company — already exceeds $4 trillion.
