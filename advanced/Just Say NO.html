💬 Useful Expressions

1. Tighten the reins
🔹 Spanish: aumentar el control, ser más estricto
🔹 Example: “When my teen started staying out late, I had to tighten the reins on their curfew.”

2. Play to someone's strengths
🔹 Spanish: aprovechar las fortalezas de alguien
🔹 Example: “Good teachers play to their students' strengths instead of focusing on weaknesses.”

3. Tap into (something)
🔹 Spanish: aprovechar, acceder a (un recurso, cualidad)
🔹 Example: “Good managers know how to tap into their team's potential.”

4. Backfire
🔹 Spanish: tener el efecto contrario, salir mal
🔹 Example: “Scare tactics can backfire and make teens more rebellious.”

🚫 Just Say NO! – A pediatrician's advice on talking to your teen about drugs and alcohol

📱 On mobile: Tap and hold highlighted words to see translation

After years of talking with teens and their parents about substance use, here's the single most important thing I've learned: Connection matters more than control. I hear it from parents all the time: “I'm scared my teen might be using drugs, but I don't know how to bring it up.” These fears are valid.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 1

Connection

🔹 the relationship or bond between people

/ la conexión, el vínculo /
Example: Building a strong connection with your teen is more effective than strict rules.
→ Construir una conexión fuerte con tu hijo es más efectivo que las reglas estrictas.

Although most teens don't use drugs or alcohol, some do. Adolescence is a time of exploration and risk-taking; and today's substances—from high-potency cannabis to counterfeit pills and weed laced with fentanyl—are far more dangerous than in previous decades. And here's the good news: You have more influence than you think. Here, I'll share what I often tell parents in my clinic. This is practical advice drawn from research and my experience having real-life conversations to help you keep your teen safe, supported and informed.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 2

Adolescence

🔹 the period of life between childhood and adulthood (roughly 12–18)

/ la adolescencia /
Example: During adolescence, teens often seek independence and new experiences.
→ Durante la adolescencia, los jóvenes suelen buscar independencia y nuevas experiencias.
high-potency

🔹 very strong or powerful, especially in effect on the body

/ de alta potencia, muy concentrado /
Example: High-potency cannabis can have stronger mental effects than milder forms.
→ El cannabis de alta potencia puede tener efectos mentales más fuertes que las formas más suaves.
counterfeit

🔹 made to look like the real thing, in order to deceive

/ falsificado, imitación /
Example: Counterfeit pills can contain dangerous substances like fentanyl.
→ Las pastillas falsificadas pueden contener sustancias peligrosas como el fentanilo.
📌 Compara: counterfeit = hecho para engañar (ilegal). Fake = no real (más amplio, no siempre ilegal).
laced ⚠️ error común

🔹 mixed with a substance (often a drug)

/ mezclado con (frecuentemente una droga) /
Example: The marijuana was laced with fentanyl.
→ La marihuana fue mezclada con fentanilo.
⚠️ Nota: Siempre se usa con "with" — laced with [algo]. No se usa "laced" solo.

When parents worry about substance use, the first instinct is often to tighten the reins: take away a teen's phone, search their room and threaten punishment. But research and experience show that what teens need most is connection, not control. Instead of jumping right to discipline, start a conversation. It's best to have these talks early and often, long before you're worried that your teen has come home after using drugs or alcohol.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 3

tighten the reins

🔹 to increase control or restrict someone's freedom

/ aumentar el control, ser más estricto /
Example: After the incident, the manager decided to tighten the reins on overtime.
→ Después del incidente, el gerente decidió aumentar el control sobre las horas extras.

Ask open-ended questions like, “What have you heard about vaping at school?” or “How do your friends feel about drinking?” When teens feel judged, they shut down. But when they feel heard, they often open up. Trust is protective. Scare tactics, on the other hand, can backfire. They can damage communication and make teens less likely to come to you when they're in trouble.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 4

vaping

🔹 inhaling vapor from electronic cigarettes or similar devices

/ vapeo, uso de cigarrillos electrónicos /
Example: Vaping has become increasingly popular among teenagers in recent years.
→ El vapeo se ha vuelto cada vez más popular entre los adolescentes en los últimos años.
scare tactics

🔹 methods that use fear to persuade someone to do or not do something

/ tácticas de miedo /
Example: Scare tactics often fail because teens see through them.
→ Las tácticas de miedo a menudo fallan porque los adolescentes las ven claramente.
backfire

🔹 to have the opposite result from what was intended

/ tener el efecto contrario, salir mal /
Example: His attempt to embarrass her backfired and made him look foolish.
→ Su intento de avergonzarla tuvo el efecto contrario y lo hizo parecer tonto.

Substance use looks different now than it did a generation ago. While alcohol is still the most commonly used substance among teens, cannabis use—especially in the form of vapes or edibles—is increasingly common. Nicotine vaping has also surged in recent years. Staying informed about what teens are actually using can help you have more meaningful conversations.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 5

edibles

🔹 food items that contain cannabis or other drugs

/ comestibles (con cannabis) /
Example: Edibles can be misleading because the effects take longer to appear.
→ Los comestibles pueden ser engañosos porque los efectos tardan más en aparecer.
📌 Nota: En EE.UU., edibles a menudo significa comida con cannabis. En otros contextos, puede significar simplemente "comida".
Nicotine

🔹 a highly addictive chemical substance found in tobacco

/ la nicotina /
Example: Nicotine addiction is hard to break, even for young people.
→ La adicción a la nicotina es difícil de romper, incluso para los jóvenes.

Sometimes, it's hard to tell whether a teen is experimenting or truly struggling. If you notice changes in mood, sleep, appetite, school performance or friendships, bring it up with your pediatrician. Pediatricians are trained to screen for substance use and mental health concerns. In addition, teens often feel more comfortable talking to a doctor than to a parent.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 6

pediatrician

🔹 a doctor who specializes in treating children and adolescents

/ el pediatra /
Example: Our pediatrician gave us helpful advice on talking to our teen.
→ Nuestro pediatra nos dio consejos útiles sobre cómo hablar con nuestro adolescente.

One of the most important goals in adolescence is delaying substance use for as long as possible. The earlier a teen starts using substances, the more likely they are to develop a substance use disorder later in life. That said, the goal shouldn't be perfection—it's protection. Even if your teen has experimented, you can still make a difference.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 7

disorder

🔹 a medical condition or illness that disrupts normal function

/ el trastorno, la enfermedad /
Example: Substance use disorder is a treatable medical condition.
→ El trastorno por uso de sustancias es una condición médica tratable.

Be curious, and ask questions like, “How might you respond if someone at a party offers you a drink?” Play to a teen's strengths. For example, if your teen has an independent streak, tap into that. You might say, “You've never been someone who follows the crowd—how do you think that could help you make decisions if friends start using drugs or alcohol?”

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 8

play to a teen's strengths

🔹 to emphasize or use a person's positive qualities

/ aprovechar las fortalezas de alguien /
Example: Good leaders play to their team's strengths instead of focusing on weaknesses.
→ Los buenos líderes aprovechan las fortalezas de su equipo en lugar de centrarse en las debilidades.
⚠️ No confundir con: play up to someone (= adular, halagar para ganar favor). Son opuestos en significado.
streak

🔹 a distinct quality or tendency in someone's character

/ el rasgo, la tendencia (de personalidad) /
Example: She has a competitive streak that drives her to succeed.
→ Tiene un rasgo competitivo que la impulsa a tener éxito.
📌 Nota: Streak también puede significar una serie (ej. a 3-day streak). Aquí significa una tendencia de personalidad — un sinónimo más formal es tendency.
tap into

🔹 to get something useful out of something that is already there but hasn't been used yet

/ aprovechar, acceder a (un recurso, cualidad) /
Example: The coach knew how to tap into the team's energy and motivation.
→ El entrenador sabía cómo aprovechar la energía y motivación del equipo.
💡 Imagina: perforar un árbol para obtener savia. = obtener algo útil de algo que ya está ahí.

The most powerful prevention tool you have is your ongoing relationship with your teen. Don't worry about giving one perfect lecture. What matters most is having lots of small conversations over time. Talk during a car ride, while doing dishes, over Sunday family breakfast or at bedtime. Be curious. Be calm. Let them know you're always there to talk—about anything. Even if your teen has already experimented, it's never too late to help guide them.

✨ No hay palabras nuevas en este párrafo. Idea clave: la comunicación constante y tranquila genera confianza.

There is a lot of positive messaging about alcohol and drugs on TV and in social media; sometimes you might be the only voice sharing that you think it's important not to use substances. Make a clear recommendation that you don't want your teen to use. You might say, “I care about your health and safety. I don't want you using alcohol or other drugs while your brain is still developing.” Teens may roll their eyes—but research shows they still hear you.

📚 Vocabulary – Paragraph 10

roll their eyes

🔹 to show annoyance or disbelief by turning the eyes upward

/ poner los ojos en blanco /
Example: My teen always rolls their eyes when I give advice, but they do listen.
→ Mi adolescente siempre pone los ojos en blanco cuando le doy consejos, pero sí escucha.
