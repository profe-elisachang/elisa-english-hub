🛤️ Dealing with Regret

What to do when you've made mistakes

Regret helps you learn from mistakes, but it's hard to learn lessons from regrets about things you didn't do. You can address this kind of regret by changing your thoughts.

💬 Useful Expressions

I have mixed feelings about…Tengo sentimientos encontrados sobre…"I have mixed feelings about turning down the transfer to Taipei."
Looking back, I wish I had…Mirando atrás, desearía haber…"Looking back, I wish I had asked for more training."
It could be argued that…Se podría argumentar que…"It could be argued that not acting was also a decision."
What I take from that is…Lo que me llevo de eso es…"What I take from that is that I value learning."

🛤️ Dealing with Regret

We all make more decisions throughout our lives than we can count. Some of those decisions bring us great happiness, while others leave us disappointed. The choices that don't turn out as we envisioned can lead to regret.

turn out phrasal verb
To end up in a particular way.
🇲🇽 resultar (de cierta manera)
"The safety drill didn't turn out as planned."
envision verb
To picture something as a future possibility.
🇲🇽 imaginar, visualizar
"We envisioned a zero-incident year."
lead to phrasal verb
To cause something to happen.
🇲🇽 llevar a, provocar
"Skipping backups can lead to data loss."

Regret comes in two main forms. Sometimes people regret specific actions, like losing their temper and yelling at a loved one. Other times people regret things they didn't do – like a career path they didn't pursue or a person they never dated, etc. (et cetera). Many people struggle more with this second type of regret because it is harder to learn from and resolve. Often we can't even know with certainty that the decision was a mistake, yet we still find ourselves wondering if we should have done something different.

lose your temper idiom
To suddenly become very angry.
🇲🇽 perder los estribos
"He lost his temper during the budget meeting."
pursue verb
To follow a goal or career.
🇲🇽 seguir, dedicarse a
"She pursued a career in HR."
date verb
To have romantic meetings with someone.
🇲🇽 salir con alguien
"They dated for a year before moving to Taiwan."
struggle with phrasal verb
To find something very difficult.
🇲🇽 batallar con
"Many staff struggle with the new system."

EXTRAActing and not acting

One study found that regrets about actions tend to get less intense over time. But regrets about things people didn't do tend to get worse with time. This might be because it is easier to learn from something one did wrong than from something one did not do at all.

tend to expression
To usually do or be something.
🇲🇽 soler, tender a
"Accidents tend to happen when people rush."
get worse phrasal verb
To become less good.
🇲🇽 empeorar
"The network problem got worse after the update."
over time expression
Gradually, as time passes.
🇲🇽 con el tiempo
"Trust grows over time."

Regret has a purpose: to help us recognize our past mistakes and avoid making similar ones in the future. For regrets about specific actions, we can undertake to do better and take steps to repair any damage our actions caused. These steps can ease the emotional distress associated with the regrets and improve our lives by helping us avoid future mistakes. But if the regret involves something we didn't do, it can be harder to resolve. In those cases, all we can do is change our thinking.

undertake verb
To promise or start to do something.
🇲🇽 emprender, comprometerse a
"We undertook to fix the leak by Friday."
take steps expression
To act in order to solve a problem.
🇲🇽 tomar medidas
"Management took steps to reduce noise levels."
ease verb
To make a problem or pain less severe.
🇲🇽 aliviar
"A clear policy can ease employee stress."

When dealing with regret about things we did not do, we must remember that uncertainty is inherent to these types of feelings. We can't be sure if we would have been happier in a different career, if the business we didn't start would have succeeded or if we would have been happily married to someone we didn't date. We can think and think about what could have happened, but in the end we are just speculating.

inherent adjective
A natural, permanent part of something.
🇲🇽 inherente, propio de
"Some risk is inherent to chemical handling."
in the end expression
Finally, after everything is considered.
🇲🇽 al final, a fin de cuentas
"In the end, the team chose the cheaper server."
speculate verb
To guess without knowing the facts.
🇲🇽 especular
"Let's not speculate about the cause of the outage."

Further, sometimes you may have had good reasons for not taking other paths. If you understand the reason why you made your past choices, you might regret them less. Likewise, it's important to remember the positive things about your current life. No situation is perfect, so it's best to accept that good and bad often go hand in hand.

likewise adverb
In the same way; also.
🇲🇽 asimismo, igualmente
"Review the safety rules. Likewise, update the checklist."
go hand in hand idiom
To be closely connected.
🇲🇽 ir de la mano
"Good training and low turnover go hand in hand."
it's best to expression
The wisest choice is to.
🇲🇽 lo mejor es
"It's best to report a near miss immediately."

Finally, a lost opportunity you regret might still reveal something about yourself that you can act on. For example, if you regret not trying to become an artist, that shows you value creativity. Even if you cannot change your career, you need not despair of expressing your artistic side altogether. You may be able to ease the regret by finding a creative hobby.

act on phrasal verb
To do something because of information or a feeling.
🇲🇽 actuar según
"HR acted on the survey results."
despair of phrasal verb
To lose hope about something.
🇲🇽 perder la esperanza de
"Don't despair of finding a solution."
altogether adverb
Completely.
🇲🇽 por completo
"They dropped the project altogether."

Regret is painful, but if we respond to it well, we can find comfort in the present and maybe even improve our lives.

painful adjective
Causing physical or emotional hurt.
🇲🇽 doloroso
"Layoffs are painful for everyone."
respond to phrasal verb
To react to something.
🇲🇽 reaccionar ante
"How did IT respond to the incident?"
comfort noun
A feeling of relief and calm.
🇲🇽 consuelo, alivio
"She found comfort in a clear plan."

🗣️ Discussion Questions

  1. Many people read Robert Frost's "The Road Not Taken" as a celebration of bold choices. Yet the speaker says both roads were "really about the same." Is the poem about bravery, or about the stories we tell ourselves later? Support your view.
  2. The extra paragraph says regrets about things we didn't do get worse over time. Do you agree? Describe a chance you passed up, and explain how you feel about it today.
  3. Imagine you are telling the story of one big career decision twenty years from now. Would you tell it "with a sigh" or with a smile? What might you do now to change that ending?

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

1. According to the article, why is regret about things we didn't do harder to resolve?

Because we can't know what would have happened, so we are only speculating, and there is no clear mistake to repair or learn from.

2. Your colleague says, "I wish I had studied abroad. I'll never be an international person now." Using the article, what could you say?

Possible answer: "You had good reasons at the time. Also, your regret shows you value international experience. You could look for a global project, take a language course or ask about a short assignment abroad."

3. Rewrite using a useful expression: "I am happy and sad about leaving my old team."

"I have mixed feelings about leaving my old team."

4. Complete with your own idea: "If I understand why I made a choice, I might regret it less, because…"

Possible answer: "…I can see that I was doing my best with the information, time and resources I had back then."
