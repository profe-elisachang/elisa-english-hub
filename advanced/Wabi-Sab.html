🏺 The Beauty of Broken Things
by Eve Upton-Clark · Fast Company

Picture a snaggletooth, a small scar, or a birthmark. According to a growing number of designers and wellness writers, these so-called flaws share something in common: they all carry a touch of wabi-sabi. Wabi-sabi is a centuries-old Japanese aesthetic philosophy that finds beauty in imperfection and in the natural wear and tear that comes with time.

A worn wooden step, a cracked teacup, or the fine lines around someone's eyes can all be examples of it. In an era of preventative treatments designed to erase every sign of aging before it appears, some argue we could all use a little more wabi-sabi in our lives.

snaggletooth
noun
a tooth that is broken, crooked, or out of place
diente torcido o desalineado
He has a snaggletooth, but his smile is unforgettable.
aesthetic
adjective
connected with beauty and the study of beauty
estético
The office was redesigned for both comfort and aesthetic appeal.

expression
the gradual damage that happens through normal use over time

Even the best equipment shows wear and tear eventually.
preventative
adjective
done in advance to stop something from happening
preventivo
The company invested in preventative maintenance to avoid costly breakdowns.
📱 Going Viral

The concept recently found an unexpected audience on TikTok. A short audio clip taken from the animated series King of the Hill has been used in close to half a million videos. In the original scene, a character picks up a rose and remarks that it "got wabi-sabi." Users have since repurposed the sound to celebrate everything from crooked teeth to aquiline noses, reframing traits that beauty standards usually discourage as something worth embracing.

Yet as the idea spreads online, some of the nuance of the original philosophy has been lost along the way. Still, in a culture shaped by looksmaxxing and ever-changing beauty micro-trends, a trend that celebrates individuality might be a step in the right direction.

crooked

The picture frame hung crooked on the wall.
aquiline
adjective
curved like an eagle's beak, usually describing a nose

efforts to maximize or improve one's physical appearance, especially among young men

Some teens spend hours a day on looksmaxxing routines they saw online.

The old factory was repurposed as a co-working space.
🔍 Chasing an Impossible Standard

The pressure to look a certain way is not new, but writers argue it keeps intensifying. One 2019 piece famously described the rise of a single, surgically-smoothed look as "The Age of Instagram Face." Commentators now describe a newer, TikTok-driven version of the same pattern as "the age of TikTok Face." Some call this aesthetic inflation: the gradual normalization of more extreme cosmetic changes over time.

The pervasiveness of this pressure creates a feedback loop that spills over into everyday life, and researchers have found a correlation between time spent online and the desire for cosmetic surgery. If the faces you scroll past every day seem to be slowly morphing into one single look, you are not imagining it — and that may be exactly why a little more wabi-sabi is worth embracing.

pervasiveness
noun
the quality of existing everywhere or spreading widely
omnipresencia
The pervasiveness of smartphones has changed how families spend time together.
feedback loop
noun phrase
a cycle in which the results of a process influence future instances of that same process
ciclo de retroalimentación
Negative comments online can create a feedback loop of anxiety and overuse.
correlation
noun
a connection or relationship between two things
correlación
Researchers found a correlation between sleep quality and productivity.
morphing
verb (-ing)
gradually developing a new appearance or changing into something else
transformándose / mutando
The small startup was slowly morphing into a large corporation.
