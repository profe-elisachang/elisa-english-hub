🔍 2. Article: Gateway Products and the Innovation Trap

📊 When a Product Becomes a Gateway

Some products are successful not because they represent the final stage of technology, but because they help customers move toward something new. A portable video recorder, for example, gave people a convenient way to capture moments before smartphones made this function almost universal. These products can be viewed as gateway products. They solve a real problem while also preparing customers for the next technological shift.

The first smartphone was a gateway product for many digital services.

⚙️ Solving the Problem Is Not Always Enough

A gateway product can genuinely solve a customer problem and still disappear later. Fax machines reduced the delay of physical mail, but they depended on paper, phone lines, and dedicated equipment. Email eventually removed much of that scaffolding. The lesson is important: a product can be useful while the system around it is gradually becoming unnecessary.

🤝 The Power of Customer Habits

Innovation is also shaped by customer behavior. People who spend years learning a particular device may become extremely skilled at using it. They may hesitate to switch because they have invested time, money, and effort in mastering the old system. This is a form of sunk cost. For companies, this creates an interesting challenge: customer loyalty may depend more on what users have already learned than on what they actually prefer.

VOCABULARY • PARAGRAPH 3

📱 Technology Often Closes the Gap

Many successful products exist because there is a temporary technological gap. Flip cameras became useful when smartphones were not good enough at video recording. Stand-alone GPS devices became popular when phones lacked reliable navigation. Once smartphones improved, however, the reason for owning these separate devices became much weaker. The companies that succeeded understood the gap; the companies that failed to monitor it were eventually left behind.

VOCABULARY • PARAGRAPH 4

🧠 The Strategic Question

This creates a difficult question for companies developing a new product: what happens when the gap disappears? A product may be profitable today but unnecessary tomorrow. Leaders therefore need to ask whether customers would still choose the product if they could start again with today's technology. If the answer is no, the product may be functioning as a gateway rather than as a long-term destination.

🚨 Planning for the Moment the Gap Closes

A gateway product is not necessarily a bad business. The danger comes when a company mistakes temporary usefulness for permanent value. Leaders can become caught flat-footed if they continue investing in a product after the original problem has disappeared. The strongest companies treat their existing products as sources of learning and prepare the next solution before competitors force them to change.

💡 Key Business Idea: A Bridge Is Not the Destination

A gateway product can be extremely valuable even if it eventually disappears. The strategic mistake is not creating a temporary product. The mistake is assuming that temporary demand will last forever.

Ask yourself: Is our product solving a permanent problem, or is it helping customers survive a temporary technological gap?

🗣️ 3. Discussion Questions

🧩 4. Self-Practice Questions

1. What is a “gateway product”?
A gateway product is a product that solves a current customer problem while helping customers move toward a newer technology or solution.
2. Why might customers hesitate to switch to a new product?
They may have invested significant time, money, and effort in learning the old system. These investments can create switching resistance and sunk costs.
3. Complete the sentence: “If a company does not monitor technological changes, it may ______.”
Possible answer: be caught flat-footed / be left behind.
4. What is the main strategic warning in this article?
Companies should not confuse temporary usefulness with permanent value. They need to monitor the technological gap and prepare for the moment when customers no longer need the existing product.
