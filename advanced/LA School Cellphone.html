📱 LA School Cellphone Ban: Year One

One city's quest to remove cellphones from public schools*

Section 1 The Ban

Before the Los Angeles Unified School District [LAUSD] cellphone ban took effect in February 2025, the use of mobile phones was ubiquitous on campuses. Not anymore.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 1

took effect

Became official; started to be applied.

entró en vigor

The new safety regulation took effect on January 1st.
ubiquitous

Seeming to be everywhere; very common.

omnipresente

Smartphones are ubiquitous in modern manufacturing facilities.

To enforce the new policy, University Charter High School—and about 250 other LAUSD schools—have turned to a local company: Yondr, the maker of a lockable pouch commonly used at film premieres and live shows to foster a distraction-free atmosphere.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 2

foster

To encourage or promote the development of something.

fomentar

Good leadership fosters a culture of safety in the workplace.
distraction-free

Without anything that takes your attention away.

libre de distracciones

The engineering team needs a distraction-free environment to focus on complex calculations.

The pouches, which are sealed with a magnet, are the most popular choice among schools to enforce the new policy. Some schools have given teachers cubbies where students deposit their devices; others simply require them to be powered down and stowed. Yet, if there is a symbol of the crackdown, it is Yondr's gray neoprene pouch.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 3

Put or packed away tidily in a space.

All tools must be stowed in their designated places after the shift.
crackdown

Strict actions taken to deal with a problem.

represión, medidas enérgicas

There has been a crackdown on unauthorized cellphone use in the cleanroom.

Section 2 Mixed Reviews

But what do the students at University High, known as Uni High, think?

"It's not the best, but I think it's for the best," said Angie Mendoza, a senior. "Sometimes the lectures can be a little boring, and you have that urge to scroll on TikTok or Instagram, but ... I've been getting better grades because I've been paying more attention in class."

Indeed, students spoke furtively of the dark arts of circumvention. Some have simply told school officials they don't have a mobile phone. Others find decoys to place in their pouches, pocketing their real devices for surreptitious use throughout the school day.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 7

Then there are the various methods to break into a sealed pouch: via a magnet, with brute strength or by using a pencil to pop it open—methods revealed in a genre of YouTube and social media videos.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 8

Physical power, rather than cleverness or skill.

Sometimes you need brute strength to open a stuck valve, but usually a tool works better.

Principal Claudia Middleton said Uni High has found ways of identifying scofflaws. For example, when a student, at the morning check-in, claims to not have a cellphone, the pupil's parent or guardian is contacted and told what the teen has said. That roots out some of the rule flouters.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 9

Senior Uleses Henderson said that underclassmen may feel that the Yondr program is "random," but juniors and seniors grasp that "there has to be some type of enforcement with the phone."

Section 3 A Look into LAUSD's Future

Lennox Middle School Principal Lissett Pichardo talked about the scourge of cellphones on her campus as if it were an existential threat.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 12

scourge

A person or thing that causes great suffering or trouble.

azote, plaga

In our industry, data breaches are the scourge of modern business.

She said that upon return to in-person learning in fall 2021, after the pandemic had forced a retreat to distance learning, phones were a major contributor to an atmosphere that proved so toxic she considered leaving her job.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 13

toxic

Very harmful or unpleasant; poisonous.

A toxic work environment can lead to high employee turnover.

Students "would videotape each other [fighting], and they would AirDrop it to everyone," Pichardo said. "It was terrible. I was like, 'I'm gonna quit.'"

Section 4 Building a Better Pouch

Yondr Chief Executive Graham Dugoni isn't surprised by students' initiative to breach the pouches. His company has spoken to students so that the company can refine its pouch design. "We're not naive," said Dugoni. "We know that anything we design—and we're constantly making improvements—they're gonna keep finding different ways around it."

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 20

naive

Lacking experience or judgment; too trusting.
