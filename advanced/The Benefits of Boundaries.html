💬 Useful Expressions

Could you clarify…? — ¿Podrías aclarar…?
Example: Could you clarify the expectations for this project?

I need to set a boundary around… — Necesito establecer un límite en cuanto a…
Example: I need to set a boundary around after-hours communication.

Let’s revisit this later. — Revisemos esto más tarde.
Example: Let's revisit this later when we have more clarity.

I’m feeling overextended. — Me siento sobrecargado.
Example: I’m feeling overextended with all these urgent meetings.

⚖️ The Benefits of Boundaries

Sometimes too much freedom makes a workplace worse

by Tony Martignetti © 2025, Fast Company. Mansueto Ventures, LLC.

📱 On mobile: Tap and hold highlighted words to see translation

We've been told that the future of work is all about freedom. Companies offer flexible hours and asynchronous communication channels that we can access anytime. The promise is that fewer constraints will make us more empowered, more creative and more connected.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 1

asynchronous
Not happening at the same time or rate.
no sincrónico
We use asynchronous communication to accommodate different time zones.
constraints
Limits that restrict your freedom to do what you want.
restricciones
Time and budget constraints forced us to prioritize.
access
To be able to use or obtain something.
acceder a / tener acceso
All employees can access the company's learning platform.

Sounds great in theory, but in practice, that kind of freedom without structure often leads to chaos. Without clear boundaries, work becomes a blur. Expectations go unspoken; resentment builds; ultimately, trust erodes. The flexibility meant to liberate us begins to wear us down.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 2

erodes
Gradually weakens or destroys.
se erosiona / se debilita
Constant criticism erodes team morale over time.
liberate
To free someone from conditions that make life difficult.
liberar
Flexible hours can liberate employees from rigid schedules.
wear down
To make someone feel tired or less enthusiastic over time.
agotar / desgastar
Constant pressure can wear down even the most resilient teams.

Boundaries protect a workplace
In today's workplace, hybrid schedules, digital fatigue and high emotional load are the norm. Employees feel burned out at work, and one of the top drivers is unclear or constantly shifting expectations. Meanwhile, remote workers report difficulty disconnecting from work. A workplace without boundaries doesn't create engagement; it creates exhaustion.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 3

burned out
Extremely tired or ill from working too hard.
agotado / quemado
Many employees feel burned out after months of overtime.
engagement
Commitment and involvement in work.
compromiso / implicación
High engagement leads to better retention and productivity.
exhaustion
Extreme tiredness, usually from overwork.
agotamiento
Mental exhaustion affects decision-making and creativity.

Boundaries help the team
Many people resist the idea of boundaries because they sound rigid or limiting. But boundaries don't constrain our power; they clarify it. Boundaries reinforce trust by setting expectations, expressing what we truly value, and defining where we end and others begin.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 4

constrain
To limit or restrict.
restringir / limitar
Too many rules can constrain creativity.
reinforce
To strengthen or support.
refuerzan
Regular check-ins reinforce team alignment.
resist
To oppose or fight against something.
resistir / oponerse
Some employees resist change because they fear the unknown.

As workplace author Greg McKeown puts it: "Constraints don't block progress; they shape it. A deadline forces a start. A boundary protects your attention."

Setting boundaries can bring change
I once worked with the leaders of an innovative climate tech company that was scaling fast. Responsiveness was a badge of honor; Slack was always on; meetings were often urgent; and everything was an immediate "yes." But beneath the surface, there was extreme fatigue, frustration and blurred priorities. The culture looked high-performing on paper but felt hollow in practice.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 6

scaling
Growing or expanding rapidly.
escalando / creciendo
The startup is scaling quickly and hiring many new people.
badge of honor
Something considered a mark of pride, even if negative.
distinción / símbolo de orgullo
Working late was seen as a badge of honor in that company.
hollow
Empty or without real meaning or value.
vacío / hueco
The team's success felt hollow because everyone was exhausted.

In a facilitated session, one team member admitted, "I never know when I'm allowed to shut off. And I'm afraid that setting limits will make me look like I don't care." That moment shifted everything. The team introduced core hours for collaboration, quiet blocks for focused work, and "Focus Fridays" without meetings or Slack messages. Within weeks, their energy felt reinvigorated. Morale improved, and creativity reemerged.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 7

facilitated
Guided or made easier (a session or process).
facilitado / guiado
The meeting was facilitated by an external consultant.
shut off
To stop working or disconnect mentally.
desconectarse / apagar
It's hard to shut off after a long day of meetings.
reinvigorated
Given new energy or strength.
revitalizado
The team felt reinvigorated after the retreat.
reemerged
Appeared again after a period of absence.
reapareció
Creativity reemerged when the pressure was reduced.

How to build healthy boundaries
Before you can communicate boundaries to others, you need to define them for yourself. What lines can't be crossed—whether that's after-hours communication, personal ethics or feedback norms? Clarity starts within, then becomes a shared agreement. Articulate your boundaries early. Don't assume others know. Share them out loud, and revisit them when your context shifts. Setting boundaries isn't a one-time conversation; it's a leadership habit that evolves alongside your role and relationships.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 8

articulate
To express clearly in words.
articular / expresar claramente
She articulated her concerns about the new policy.
revisit
To consider or discuss something again.
revisar / volver a considerar
We should revisit the policy every quarter.
evolves
Develops gradually over time.
evoluciona
Leadership styles evolve as teams grow and change.

Leave room for a buffer. Not every line is absolute. Build a "pause zone" for conversation and recalibration. This could mean committing to a 30-minute delay before responding to non-urgent messages or setting up clear blocks in the week for focused work versus collaborative time. A boundary that protects your time but violates your purpose won't hold. Make sure your boundaries serve your bigger "why." The most sustainable boundaries are those that feel not only practical but deeply principled.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 9

buffer
A protective space or margin.
amortiguador / margen
We should build a buffer into our schedule for unexpected tasks.
recalibration
The act of adjusting or correcting something.
recalibración / reajuste
The team needs a recalibration of its goals after the merger.
violates
Breaks or disrespects (a rule, agreement, or value).
viola / quebranta
A policy that violates employee well-being is not sustainable.
sustainable
Able to be maintained over time.
sostenible / duradero
We need sustainable solutions that don't burn people out.

Sometimes boundaries need fixing
Resentment, anxiety and exhaustion are often signs that a boundary has been breached or needs reinforcement. Your emotional patterns are data—listen to them before they become burnout. Evaluate and evolve. What worked last year may not work now. Boundaries should shift with your life, your leadership and your team. Revisit them regularly and treat them as a living part of how you work, not a fixed set of rules.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 10

Broken (a law, rule, or agreement).

The contract was breached when deadlines were missed.
reinforcement
The act of strengthening or supporting.
refuerzo
Positive reinforcement encourages good behavior.
evaluate
To judge or assess the value or quality of something.
evaluar
We need to evaluate our processes regularly.

Boundaries and connection
Boundaries don't diminish connection. They deepen it because they make it safe to be fully present. Start by asking yourself and your team these questions: Where in our work are we overextended, unclear or always on? What's one boundary you need to set or revisit? Where is so-called "freedom" creating confusion? In work cultures without boundaries, people don't thrive. They scatter and self-protect, and eventually they burn out. But when leaders and teams get clear on their edges—on what's okay and what's not—they build something far more powerful: trust, clarity, creative energy and a deeper sense of shared purpose.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 11

Trying to do too much, causing problems or stress.
sobrecargados / extendidos
We are overextended with too many simultaneous projects.
thrive
To grow, develop, or be successful.
prosperar / desarrollarse
People thrive in environments where they feel safe and valued.
scatter
To spread out in different directions.
dispersarse
When trust is broken, team members scatter and self-protect.
shared purpose
A common goal or reason for working together.
propósito compartido
A shared purpose unites the team and drives collaboration.
