📊 From Fans to Foes

For these YouTubers, online fame draws intense love and hate

Adapted from an article by Rachel Hutton, The Minnesota Star Tribune (2026). Text rewritten for classroom use.

Three Minnesota influencers, John O’Sullivan, Josh Liljenquist, and Chris Ostroushko, recently learned how fickle internet fame can be. One day, they were racking up thousands of likes. The next day, they were buried under a dogpile of angry comments. Each one had built an audience in a different way: a tour guide, a self-appointed do-gooder, and an unlikely activist, respectively. The activist’s rant at his first protest went viral.

fickleadj.

Changing often and without warning.

inconstante

Client interest in safety training is fickle; it spikes after an accident.

dogpilen.

A sudden, large group attack on one person, usually online.

ataque masivo

One mistake in the HR newsletter led to a dogpile in the company chat.

respectivelyadv.

In the same order as the items you just listed.

respectivamente, en ese orden

Ana, Luis, and Rosa work in EHS, HR, and IT, respectively.

rantn./v.

A long, angry, uncontrolled speech or complaint.

diatriba, desahogo furioso

His rant about the slow VPN was posted on the IT forum.

Their latest behavior set off a wave of criticism. One man’s explanation for leaving the state sounded tone-deaf. Another was accused of assault after a video went viral. A third faced questions about burnishing his image by filming downtrodden people who were receiving free help.

tone-deafadj.

Not aware of how other people feel or what they need.

insensible, fuera de lugar

An email about “fun team games” during layoffs felt tone-deaf.

burnishv.

To improve a reputation or image through effort.

pulir, mejorar (la imagen)

The company posted safety awards to burnish its public image.

downtroddenadj.

Treated badly by people with more power.

oprimido, marginado

Auditors found that night-shift workers felt downtrodden.

Quick judgments

When influencers wade into controversy, audiences often judge them from just a few facts about what transpired. Then online algorithms can boost the animus, even among people who used to be loyal fans.

wade intophr. v.

To enter a difficult situation, often with effort or risk.

meterse en (un asunto difícil)

I don’t want to wade into the argument about badge policy.

transpirev.

To happen, especially when facts slowly become known.

ocurrir, suceder

We still don’t know what transpired during the data breach.

animusn.

A strong feeling of dislike or hatred.

animosidad

There was real animus between the two departments.

Intimacy at a distance

According to one media researcher, our brains react to a face on a screen almost like a face in real life. So viewers form parasocial bonds: one-sided relationships with people they have never met. This feels especially real when influencers address the camera directly, as if they were talking to one viewer. Fans enjoy their success in a vicarious way, and the strong feelings spread through contagion.

addressv.

To speak directly to a person or group; also, to start dealing with a problem.

dirigirse a; abordar (un problema)

The plant manager addressed the night shift directly about the new safety rules.

parasocialadj.

A one-sided relationship where you feel close to someone who doesn’t know you.

parasocial (relación unilateral)

Employees may feel a parasocial bond with a famous CEO.

vicariousadj.

Felt by watching or reading about someone else’s experience.

indirecto, a través de otro

I take vicarious pride when our plant wins a global award.

contagionn.

A feeling or attitude that spreads quickly between people.

contagio

Panic spread by contagion after the false fire alarm.

A love/hate relationship

If someone you like does something wrong, you may condone it, or your brain may feel cognitive dissonance. When a celebrity has a fall from grace, it can even feel comforting, because nobody is perfect. But when an influencer feels like a friend, bad behavior can feel like a personal betrayal.

condonev.

To accept or overlook behavior that is wrong.

tolerar, justificar

Our code of conduct does not condone harassment of any kind.

cognitive dissonancen.

The discomfort of holding two conflicting beliefs.

disonancia cognitiva

I felt cognitive dissonance: I trust this vendor, but its audit failed.

fall from graceidiom

To lose respect or status, usually after doing something wrong.

caer en desgracia

The director’s fall from grace began with the safety cover-up.

betrayaln.

Breaking someone’s trust in a way that hurts them.

traición

Employees called the data leak a betrayal of their trust.

Profiting from negativity

Algorithms exploit human nature. When people feel angry or threatened by a video, they are more likely to stay on the platform, comment, and share. Those engagement behaviors increase profits for the companies, so anger becomes part of the business model.

exploitv.

To use something or someone unfairly for your own benefit.

aprovechar, explotar

Phishing emails exploit people’s fear of losing access.

stay onphr. v.

To continue using or being in a place or system.

seguir en, permanecer

Please stay on the call until the safety briefing ends.

engagementn.

Likes, comments, and shares; active participation online.

interacción, participación

Our internal newsletter has low engagement from night shifts.

🗣️ Discussion Questions

  1. Why do we feel so personally hurt when a public figure we never met behaves badly? Is that reaction rational?
  2. Should leaders at a company be held to a higher standard in their private lives than regular employees? Where is the line?
  3. Do social media algorithms make us angrier, or do they just show us what we already feel? Who is responsible?

✏️ Self-Practice

1. Complete: “Our code of conduct does not ______ retaliation against people who report problems.”
condone. It means to accept or overlook wrong behavior.
2. Which word fits? “Sending a party invitation during the plant shutdown was ______.”
tone-deaf. The sender did not understand how others felt.
3. Explain in your own words: What is a parasocial relationship? Give an example from your own life.
A one-sided bond with someone who does not know you, such as feeling close to a YouTuber, a CEO, or an athlete you follow. Your example may vary.
4. Choose the best answer: “I felt ______ when I learned my favorite brand had lied.” (a) vicarious pride (b) cognitive dissonance (c) contagion
(b) cognitive dissonance. You liked the brand, but the lie conflicts with that feeling.
