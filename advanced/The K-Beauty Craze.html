💬 Discussion Questions

  • 1. In your industry, what are the advantages of being a “first mover” or launching products with exceptional speed, like K-beauty brands?
    📌 Suggested Answer (click)

    Being a first mover allows a company to capture market share, build brand recognition quickly, and set customer expectations. However, speed also risks lower quality. In manufacturing, fast adaptation to trends can be a huge competitive edge.

  • 2. How do you think smaller companies can compete against big manufacturers while maintaining originality?
    📌 Suggested Answer (click)

    Smaller companies can focus on niche markets, personalized service, unique storytelling, and leveraging social media virality. They can also partner with ODM manufacturers to reduce production costs while keeping creative control.

  • 3. What risks come with a market that constantly “chases the next exciting thing”? Have you seen similar patterns in your professional field?
    📌 Suggested Answer (click)

    Risks include low customer loyalty, short product life cycles, pressure to constantly innovate, and wasted inventory. In tech or fashion, this pattern is common; brands must balance trends with core products.

  • 4. Where do women in León typically buy cosmetics when they don't go to department stores? (supermarkets, Sephora, online, OXXO, etc.)
    📌 Suggested Answer (click)

    Many women in León buy cosmetics at supermarkets (Soriana, Walmart), specialty chains like Sephora or Mary Kay, online platforms (Mercado Libre, Instagram shops), and sometimes at local beauty supply stores. OXXO convenience stores are not common for cosmetics beyond basic items.

  • 💡 Asian beauty drugstore model explained: In Japan/Korea, "drugstores" (e.g., Matsumoto Kiyoshi, Olive Young) sell hundreds of affordable cosmetics + some medicines. New items arrive weekly, customers test freely, prices are low. It’s a fast-fashion approach for beauty.
    5. Do you think a Korean-style beauty drugstore (like Olive Young, with affordable, trendy cosmetics and rapid product turnover) could succeed in León, Mexico? Why or why not? Consider prices, shopping habits, location, competition.
    📌 Suggested Answer (click)

    It could succeed in high-traffic areas or shopping malls if the store offers dramatically lower prices than Sephora and strong social media buzz. However, Mexicans are used to testing products at department stores or buying online. The walking culture in León is lower than in Seoul, so location near universities or plazas would be key. Also, local preferences for trusted international brands (L’Oréal, Maybelline) might require heavy marketing education. With the right adaptation (local brands + Korean trends), it might work.
