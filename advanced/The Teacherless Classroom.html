🏫 The Teacherless Classroom

Inside the fast-growing schools that replace teachers with AI-guided learning

A school without teachers

Blake Mohseni is one of the most fervent supporters of Alpha School, a network of private schools built around AI. There are no teachers, only "guides" who motivate students. Lessons come from AI software and last just two hours a day. The rest of the day is for hands-on workshops, from building robots to scaling obstacle courses. It all comes with a hefty price tag of $55,000 a year. Founder MacKenzie Price says the model offers more personal and efficient learning, and that AI can finally unlock human potential.

📖 Vocabulary · Paragraph 1

Mixed reviews

The model has caused both praise and skepticism. As Alpha's rollout spreads quickly, educators are weighing what AI should do in class, and the change has brought turbulence for some. Critics note there is little outside research behind the curriculum. Others fear a teacher-free classroom will sever the relationships that sit at the heart of learning. One philosophy professor worries that we are changing the very nature of education.

📖 Vocabulary · Paragraph 2

What guides actually do

The guides are not trained teachers. They supervise students and act as mentors, and later in the day they steer workshops and help set individual goals. Price insists, "We're not replacing teachers; the role is just changing." Even so, a Stanford professor, Victor Lee, accepts that AI can streamline some tasks but warns against cutting out teachers altogether. Teachers do so much that is beyond what even advanced language models can do.

📖 Vocabulary · Paragraph 3

Preference for the privileged?

Because Alpha charges tuition, it tends to cater to families of a certain income level. Price says doubts about Alpha reflect a perennial resistance to new ideas in education. She admits there is some selection bias, since wealthier students often have more resources, but she says she wants to change that. At the flagship school in Texas, over 40% of students receive financial aid. Lee, however, questions whether private schools really try to educate more people.

📖 Vocabulary · Paragraph 4

Growing amid debate

Alpha's growth is unfurling in the middle of the national debate over school choice. Supporters say it gives parents more options and gives low-income students access to better schooling. Price points to families from across the political spectrum, and almost a dozen new campuses opened this fall because of surging demand. Mohseni sums it up: at the end of the day, the best asset he can give his daughter is her brain, and AI is where the future is heading.

✍️ Self-Practice Questions

1. Which word means "to cut a connection or relationship"?
Sever. "The audit severed our relationship with that supplier."
2. Complete: "A new office policy can ______ the hiring process, saving us hours each week."
Streamline. It means to make something work more simply and effectively.
3. What is the difference between "fervent" and "hefty"?
Fervent describes strong, sincere feeling (a fervent supporter). Hefty describes a large amount, especially money (a hefty fee).
4. Explain "a perennial problem" with an example from your workplace.
A problem that keeps coming back, e.g. "Late safety reports are a perennial problem every quarter." Answers will vary.
