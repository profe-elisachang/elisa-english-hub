Lab-Grown Organs: How Far Should Science Go?

Today's article: scientists have grown miniature human hearts and livers complete with their own blood vessels — a major step toward lab-made organs.

📄 Read the article handout / screen — use the list below to look up any words you don't know

Before reading: Have you ever heard of "lab-grown" organs or tissue before? What was your first reaction when you saw the headline?

🗣️ Debate & Critical Discussion

🔴 Core — always ask
  • Is it ethical to grow human organ tissue in a lab? Where do you think the line should be drawn?
  • Medical technology often moves faster than regulation and public understanding. Is that a risk worth taking?
  • If lab-grown organs eventually become common, how might that change the way people think about the human body?
🟢 Extend — if time allows
  • Breakthroughs like this often take decades to actually reach patients. Do you think the public is too impatient with science?
  • Could advances like this widen inequality — for example, if only wealthy countries or patients benefit first?
  • Overall, does this discovery excite you or concern you more? Why?

✅ Quick Comprehension Check (optional)

1. True or False: Scientists have already achieved organs with real blood actually circulating through them.
False — the vessels formed, but blood is not yet circulating. That's still a future goal.
2. Besides the heart, what other organ did the team also grow using the same method?
The liver — they created vascularized mini-livers using the same approach.
