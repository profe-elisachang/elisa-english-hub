📱 Child Privacy Suffers in the Age of Sharenting

Recently, a New Taipei City couple who are family influencers posted a video of their young child defecating in her diaper behind a UNIQLO clothing rack. Widely condemned as a scheme for online attention, the act drew police involvement and raised concerns about privacy and parenting.

Absorbent garment worn by babies or young children

Parents should change diapers frequently to maintain the child's comfort and hygiene.

Expressed strong disapproval of something

The company's unethical practices were widely condemned by industry regulators.

A plan or arrangement, often with negative connotations

esquema, plan

The marketing team developed a comprehensive scheme to increase brand awareness.

This example shows the darker aspects of sharenting, a term describing how some parents excessively share their children's lives or photos online. Sharenting thrives on social media, where engaging content is rewarded with likes, followers, and revenue from sponsorships and advertisements. The way in which parents profit from intimate childhood moments has fueled the rapid growth of family influencer accounts and blurred the boundary between genuine care and the pursuit of popularity.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 2

Accompanying this allure are formidable risks. Foremost among them is the loss of privacy, as parents meticulously document and widely disseminate the lives of their children who are too young to consent. With privacy lost, the boundaries indispensable for a child's well-being may also weaken.

Experts warn of long-term psychological consequences, including identity confusion, anxiety, and the difficulty of developing an authentic sense of self under constant public scrutiny. Furthermore, embarrassing childhood moments may remain online permanently, affecting future relationships and opportunities.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 4

scrutiny

Critical observation or examination

escrutinio, examen detallado

The financial reports underwent intense scrutiny from the auditing team.

Recognizing these issues, several nations have initiated legislation. France leads global efforts, allowing children to sue their parents for sharing their photos online without consent once they reach adulthood. In 2020, France went further by enacting a statute that requires government authorization before children can appear in commercial online videos, with rigorous regulations on their working hours. Illinois, US, has also passed a law to protect children's rights, requiring that child influencers receive a share of income from their video blogs, with their earnings secured in a trust fund until they turn 18. As more nations address sharenting, it is time for society to question whether the focus should be on the child or the parent holding the camera.

1. Fill in the blank:
The company __________ inspected every shipment to ensure quality standards were met.
(Use a word from the vocabulary that means "very carefully")
2. True or False:
According to the article, "sharenting" refers to parents carefully protecting their children's privacy online.
3. Matching:
Match the word with its meaning:
a) scrutiny
b) formidable
c) disseminate

1) To spread information widely
2) Critical examination
3) Inspiring fear or respect through power
4. Short answer:
What are two risks mentioned in the article about children whose parents share their photos extensively online?
Reference Answers
  1. meticulously - The company meticulously inspected every shipment to ensure quality standards were met.
  2. False - Sharenting refers to parents excessively sharing their children's lives or photos online, often without considering privacy concerns.
  3. a-2 (scrutiny = critical examination), b-3 (formidable = inspiring fear or respect), c-1 (disseminate = spread information widely)
  4. Two risks include: (1) Loss of privacy and inability to consent, (2) Long-term psychological consequences such as identity confusion and anxiety, (3) Embarrassing moments remaining online permanently affecting future opportunities. (Any two are acceptable)
