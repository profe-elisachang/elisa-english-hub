Developing Countries Are Being Priced Out, in Struggle for Affordable Finance

Section 2 & 3

📰 Article with Vocabulary

💡 On mobile: tap and hold highlighted words to see translation. On desktop: hover over them.

Developing countries are being priced out of the affordable finance they desperately need for sustainable development, with sovereign credit ratings often overstating risk and overlooking long-term economic potential, the UN said on Monday.

A credit rating is an assessment of how likely a borrower, such as a government, is to repay its debt on time and in full. For sovereign states, ratings influence how much countries pay to borrow in international markets: the lower the rating, the higher the perceived risk and usually the higher the interest costs.

The current system too often relies on "outdated and incomplete information", leaving countries unfairly penalised in global capital markets, the deputy UN chief Amina Mohammed told the opening of the UN's Economic and Social Council, ECOSOC, Special Meeting on Credit Ratings, delivering remarks on behalf of Secretary-General António Guterres.

📚 Vocabulary — Paragraphs 1 & 2

priced out

When something becomes too expensive for you to afford or compete in

excluido por el precio

Small factories are being priced out of raw materials because of global inflation.
sovereign credit rating

An official score that tells investors how risky it is to lend money to a country

calificación crediticia soberana

A low sovereign credit rating means a country pays more interest when it borrows money.
overstating

Making something seem bigger, worse, or more important than it really is

sobreestimando / exagerando

The report was overstating the safety risks, which caused unnecessary concern.
assessment

A careful evaluation or judgment of something

evaluación

HR performs a performance assessment every six months.
borrower

A person, company, or country that takes money as a loan and must pay it back

prestatario / deudor

Developing countries are the largest borrowers in international financial markets.
penalised

Put at a disadvantage or made to suffer unfairly

penalizados / castigados

Workers are penalised for arriving late more than three times a month.
capital markets

Financial markets where governments and companies raise large amounts of money through bonds and stocks

mercados de capital

Access to capital markets allows countries to fund infrastructure without raising taxes immediately.

"Adequate and timely finance is the fuel that drives sustainable development — today that fuel is running perilously low, and it's getting more costly."

— Amina Mohammed, Deputy UN Secretary-General

She pointed to nearly $1.4 trillion in annual debt servicing costs across developing countries, while more than 3.4 billion people live in countries that spend more on debt interest payments than on health or education.

📚 Vocabulary — The Finance Paragraph

adequate

Enough in quantity or quality; sufficient for the purpose

adecuado / suficiente

We need to ensure adequate safety training before the new equipment arrives.
perilously

In a way that is dangerously close to a serious problem

peligrosamente

The project budget was running perilously low by the third quarter.
debt servicing costs

The money a borrower must regularly pay to cover loan interest and partial repayment

costos del servicio de la deuda

High debt servicing costs left the government unable to invest in public hospitals.
Global Instability

Ms. Mohammed added that global instability is deepening the crisis. Rising fuel and raw material costs linked to conflict and economic volatility are intensifying fiscal pressures and slowing growth, while climate-vulnerable countries continue to face disaster losses without access to affordable recovery financing.

"This is a matter of profound importance," Ms. Mohammed said.

📚 Vocabulary — Global Instability

economic volatility

Unpredictable and rapid changes in economic conditions — prices, growth, exchange rates

volatilidad económica

Economic volatility makes it hard for companies to plan long-term investments.
fiscal pressures

Stress on a government's budget — when spending needs exceed available revenue

presiones fiscales / presupuestarias

Fiscal pressures forced the company to freeze hiring for the rest of the year.
intensifying

Becoming stronger, more serious, or more extreme over time

intensificándose / agravándose

The competition for talent in the tech sector is intensifying every year.
Debt Reform Efforts Broaden

Ms. Mohammed also linked the credit ratings debate to wider efforts to reform the global debt architecture and pointed to new steps aimed at giving developing countries a stronger voice in debt discussions.

These include a borrowers' platform, work on principles for responsible sovereign borrowing and lending, and a UN-led process bringing together debtor and creditor countries, private creditors, international financial institutions, academics and civil society.

She also cited the planned African Credit Rating Agency as an example of efforts to improve data, transparency and risk assessment.

📚 Vocabulary — Debt Reform

debt architecture

The overall system and rules that govern how countries borrow, lend, and restructure debt globally

arquitectura de la deuda

Reforming the global debt architecture could give poorer nations more negotiating power.
creditor

A person, bank, or country that has lent money and expects repayment

acreedor

The creditor nations demanded stricter repayment conditions before offering new loans.
debtor

A person, company, or country that owes money to another

deudor

Debtor countries often have little power in negotiations with large international banks.
transparency

Openness — being clear and honest about how decisions are made and data is used

transparencia

Greater transparency in the rating process would help countries understand why their score changed.
civil society

Non-government organizations, community groups, and citizens acting together for public interest

sociedad civil

Civil society groups pushed for better environmental standards in the new trade agreement.
Call to Reimagine Ratings

Ms. Mohammed urged a major shift in how sovereign ratings are designed, arguing that assessments should capture not only vulnerability, but also opportunity.

"We must transform the mindsets from long-term speculation to long-term investment,"

— Amina Mohammed

She called for broader, more transparent and forward-looking methodologies that better reflect countries' real prospects.

Ms. Mohammed stressed that affordable borrowing for development can strengthen a country's future solvency.

Investment in health, education, infrastructure, climate resilience and renewable energy, she said, can generate prosperity, reduce risk and improve economic stability over time.

She also criticised narrow measures of progress, insisting that "GDP tells us the cost of everything and the value of very little."

Ms. Mohammed called for greater accountability from governments, investors and ratings providers alike, alongside stronger data and fairer methodologies.

"It's time to turn credit ratings from barriers into contributors to long-term finance and sustainable development."

— Amina Mohammed, urging a new approach that helps developing countries secure the financing they need.

📚 Vocabulary — Call to Reimagine

forward-looking

Focused on the future; thinking ahead rather than relying only on past data

con visión de futuro / prospectivo

A forward-looking HR strategy considers future skills needs, not just current headcount.
methodology

A system of methods and rules used to carry out a process or reach a conclusion

metodología

The safety audit methodology needs to be updated to cover remote work risks.
solvency

The ability to pay all your debts; being financially stable in the long term

solvencia

Investing in employee wellbeing can actually improve a company's long-term solvency.
accountability

Being responsible for your actions and decisions, and having to explain them to others

rendición de cuentas / responsabilidad

Greater accountability in procurement decisions reduced corruption significantly.
vulnerability

The state of being exposed to risk or harm; weakness that can be exploited

vulnerabilidad

The IT team identified a critical vulnerability in the company's network security.
prospects

The likelihood of future success; possibilities for what may happen

perspectivas / prospectos

The company's growth prospects improved significantly after the new product launch.

Section — Language Focus

🔑 Key Sentence Patterns from the Article

These patterns appear in the article. Notice how they work — you already know them, but do you use them naturally when expressing complex ideas?

  • Proportional Relationship
    "The lower the rating, the higher the perceived risk and usually the higher the interest costs."
    → The + comparative, the + comparative. Use this to show that two things change together. Common in economics, engineering, and management discussions.
  • Contrast: Not only X, but also Y
    "Assessments should capture not only vulnerability, but also opportunity."
    → Use this to argue for a wider perspective — that something is being looked at too narrowly. Very useful when challenging a limited view in a meeting.
  • Cause → Unintended Consequence
    "...leaving countries unfairly penalised in global capital markets."
    → [action/situation], leaving [someone] [result]. The "-ing" form shows what the consequence is, often negative and unintended. Great for incident reports and root cause analysis.
  • Linking Two Problems
    "Rising fuel and raw material costs linked to conflict and economic volatility are intensifying fiscal pressures and slowing growth."
    → Notice how multiple causes connect to multiple effects in one sentence. This is how analysts and senior managers talk. Practice building these compound sentences.
  • Reframing the Narrative
    "It's time to turn credit ratings from barriers into contributors."
    → It's time to turn [X] from [negative role] into [positive role]. Powerful for proposals and advocacy. Use it when you want to suggest a fundamental change in how something is seen.
