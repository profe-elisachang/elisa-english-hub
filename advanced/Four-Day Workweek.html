💬 Useful Expressions

run the gamut (from … to …)

abarcar toda la gama (de … a …)

The team's concerns run the gamut from minor scheduling issues to complete restructuring of shifts.

on the horizon

en el horizonte / próximo a suceder

Several changes to our production schedule are on the horizon, so please stay informed.

zoom out

ver el panorama general / alejarse para ver la situación completa

If you zoom out, you'll see that our efficiency has actually improved over the past two quarters.

throw one's clout behind something

apoyar algo con su influencia

The director decided to throw his clout behind the new flexible work proposal.

get down to + number / task

llegar a / reducir a / ponerse a (hacer algo)

With AI handling routine tasks, we might get down to a 32-hour workweek within this decade.

⏰ Is a Four-Day Workweek in Your Future?

Experts insist that the rise of AI could lead to more leisure for everyone

Paragraph 1

Concerned insiders fret about the potential downsides of AI, which run the gamut from turbocharged spam to human extinction. But what about the possible upsides of AI? If the potential problems of technology include mass economic displacement and the end of our species, then certainly the upsides have to be pretty great to justify continued progress.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 1

fret

To worry constantly about something

preocuparse / angustiarse

Many workers fret about being replaced by automated systems.
downsides

The negative aspects or disadvantages of something

desventajas / aspectos negativos

The downsides of working night shifts include fatigue and social isolation.
run the gamut

To include the full range of possibilities from one extreme to another

abarcar toda la gama

Employee feedback ran the gamut from very satisfied to completely frustrated.
turbocharged spam

Massively amplified junk or scam messages, here powered by AI

spam turbocargado / correo basura potenciado

AI tools could generate turbocharged spam that floods inboxes worldwide.
displacement

Being forced out of a job or position, usually by new technology

desplazamiento / reemplazo laboral

Economic displacement caused by automation is a serious concern for factory workers.
justify

To give a good enough reason for something

justificar

The company must justify its decision to reduce the workforce to all stakeholders.
Paragraph 2

Indeed, a chorus of experts has been singing AI's potentially miraculous benefits. These go way beyond helping students write term papers. Phenomenal medical and scientific breakthroughs and smart personal assistants that can tackle much of life's boring administration may be on the horizon, they say.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 2

singing (praises)

Praising something enthusiastically and publicly (idiom: "singing the praises of")

alabando / elogiando

The CEO has been singing the benefits of the new production system at every meeting.
breakthroughs

Important new discoveries or achievements that change what is possible

avances / descubrimientos importantes

Recent breakthroughs in battery technology could transform the electric vehicle industry.
tackle

To deal with or take on a difficult task or problem

hacerse cargo de / abordar / enfrentar

Our team needs to tackle the quality issues before the next audit.
on the horizon

Likely to happen soon; approaching in the near future

en el horizonte / próximo a suceder

Major changes to our supply chain are on the horizon, so teams should prepare.

The bots will do the grunt work for us

Paragraph 3

When lots of people hear that AI may soon be smart enough to do much of our work for us, they understandably start worrying about losing their jobs and livelihoods. But a new study from think tank Autonomy paints an alternative picture. Yes, there may soon be less work for humans. But handled correctly, that could just mean a lot more leisure for all of us.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 3

grunt work

Tedious, repetitive, or low-status work that still needs to be done

trabajo pesado / trabajo aburrido y repetitivo

Junior technicians often do the grunt work of data entry and equipment cleaning.
livelihoods

The way someone earns the money needed for food, housing, and basic needs

medios de vida / sustento

The closure of the plant threatened the livelihoods of over 2,000 local workers.
think tank

An organization of experts that researches and advises on important issues

grupo de expertos / organismo de investigación

A leading think tank published a report on the future of manufacturing jobs in Mexico.
leisure

Free time when you are not working or doing obligatory tasks

tiempo libre / ocio

Workers who have more leisure time tend to return more focused and productive.
Paragraph 4

The think tank's report "found that projected productivity gains from the introduction of AI could reduce the working week from 40 to 32 hours for 28% of the workforce—8.8 million people in Britain and 35 million in the U.S.—while maintaining pay and performance," reports the U.K. Guardian. In the U.S., 128 million workers could have their workweeks reduced by at least 10 percent.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 4

gains

Improvements or increases in performance, output, or benefit

ganancias / mejoras / avances

Efficiency gains from the new robotic line allowed the company to reduce overtime hours.
Paragraph 5

"According to Autonomy, such a policy could also help to avoid mass unemployment and reduce widespread mental and physical illnesses," the article continues.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 5

unemployment

The situation of not having a paid job

desempleo

Rising unemployment is pushing governments to invest in retraining programs.
illnesses

Diseases or conditions that affect physical or mental health

enfermedades / padecimientos

Chronic stress at work can lead to serious mental and physical illnesses over time.
Paragraph 6

If that sounds far-fetched to you, that's not unreasonable. Experts have been predicting increases in productivity would lead to shortened workweeks at least since the great economist John Maynard Keynes opined that his grandkids would work a 15-hour workweek in 1930. They've always been wrong. Up to now, when productivity has gone up, people have used those gains to earn more money and buy more stuff rather than give themselves more time off.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 6

far-fetched

Very unlikely to be true or to happen; difficult to believe

inverósimil / difícil de creer

A fully automated factory once seemed far-fetched, but it now exists.
opined

Stated one's opinion formally or publicly

opinó / expresó su punto de vista

The director opined that the new safety regulations would be difficult to implement quickly.
stuff

Informal word for things or material goods

cosas / artículos

Historically, people spend extra income on more stuff rather than taking more time off.

At least one billionaire sees a three-day week in our future

Paragraph 7

But it's not just one idealistic think tank floating the idea. This time around, we really may get a shorter workweek out of all our technological gains. The concept of a four-day workweek was percolating plenty in the public conversation well before the recent release of amazing generative AI tools got us all imagining our future robot butlers. Several four-day workweek trials, on the company and national level, all had positive results and attracted high-profile backers.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 7

floating

Suggesting or circulating an idea to test people's reactions

lanzando / poniendo a circular (una idea)

Management has been floating the idea of a flexible Friday schedule for the last month.
percolating

Spreading slowly and steadily through a group or public conversation

proliferando / filtrándose gradualmente

The idea of remote work was already percolating before the pandemic made it a necessity.
high-profile backers

Well-known and influential people or organizations that publicly support something

patrocinadores / apoyadores de alto perfil

The new labor reform attracted high-profile backers from technology and finance sectors.
Paragraph 8

Bill Gates, too, is willing to throw his considerable clout behind the idea. He thinks we might even get down to working just three days a week.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 8

clout

Power and influence, especially in business or politics

influencia / poder

Senior engineers with technical clout can shape how new systems are designed and adopted.
get down to

To reach a lower number or level; also: to finally start doing something seriously

llegar a / reducir a / ponerse a (hacer algo)

With new automation, we hope to get down to a 35-hour workweek by next year.
Paragraph 9

"If you zoom out, you know, the purpose of life is not just to do jobs," Gates says (which is slightly ironic given his well-documented workaholism in his early days). "If you eventually get a society where you only have to work three days a week or something, that's probably OK, if the machines can make all the food and the stuff and we don't have to work as hard."

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 9

zoom out

To step back and look at the bigger picture instead of focusing on small details

ver el panorama general / alejarse para ver la situación completa

If you zoom out, you'll see that our defect rate has actually improved significantly this year.
ironic

Strange or funny because it is the opposite of what you would expect

irónico

It's ironic that the manager who promoted work-life balance was always the last one to leave the office.
workaholism

The habit of working excessively and feeling unable to stop

adicción al trabajo / trabajolismo

Workaholism can lead to burnout if employees don't set proper boundaries.
Paragraph 10

Gates is careful to offer a few words of caution though. If the development of AI proceeds too quickly, he warns, society might not have time to adjust, and real suffering would occur. A positive transition also demands understanding and forethought from our leaders. But "if it proceeds at a reasonable pace and the government helps those people who have to learn new things, then it's all good," Gates concludes.

📚 Vocabulary - Paragraph 10

transition

The process of changing from one state or condition to another

transición

The transition to automated assembly lines took three years and required extensive retraining.
forethought

Careful thinking and planning done in advance

previsión / planificación anticipada

With proper forethought, the safety team avoided several hazards during the plant upgrade.
Paragraph 11

Both those conditions, particularly the need for competent politicians doing the right thing, seem like very big ifs at the moment. But assuming that scientists keep advancing and society keeps innovating in response to their progress, AI might not destroy jobs. Instead, it could destroy the traditional 40-hour workweek.

competente / capaz

🗣️ Discussion Questions

  • Do you think a four-day workweek is realistic in your industry or company? What would need to change to make it possible?
  • In your experience, when productivity increases at work, do employees tend to get more free time — or just more tasks? Why do you think that happens?
  • What do you think are the biggest risks of AI taking over certain jobs in manufacturing or engineering? How should companies handle that transition?

✏️ Self-Practice

  1. Fill in the blank with the correct word (livelihoods, far-fetched, clout, forethought, percolating):

    a) The idea of a paperless office has been ________ in our department for months.
    b) Losing their jobs would threaten the ________ of thousands of factory workers.
    c) The proposal seemed ________ at first, but the data proved it was achievable.
    d) With more ________, we could have avoided the supply chain delays.
    e) She has a lot of ________ in the industry, so her endorsement really matters.
  2. True or False?

    a) The economist John Maynard Keynes predicted a 15-hour workweek in 1930 — and he was right.
    b) According to the article, AI could reduce the working week while maintaining pay and performance.
    c) Bill Gates thinks AI will definitely destroy jobs without any negative consequences.
  3. Short answer: According to the article, what TWO conditions does Bill Gates say are necessary for AI to bring a positive transition?
  4. In your own words, explain the meaning of "zoom out" and use it in a sentence about your workplace.
Reference Answers
  1. a) percolating  |  b) livelihoods  |  c) far-fetched  |  d) forethought  |  e) clout
  2. a) False — Keynes predicted it, but it never happened; people used extra income to buy more, not work less.
    b) True.
    c) False — Gates warns that if AI moves too quickly, real suffering could occur; he sees both risks and benefits.
  3. Gates says AI must (1) proceed at a reasonable pace so society has time to adapt, and (2) be supported by governments that help people learn new skills.
  4. "Zoom out" means to step back and look at the bigger picture. Example: If you zoom out, you'll see that our defect rate has improved a lot this year despite last month's setback.
