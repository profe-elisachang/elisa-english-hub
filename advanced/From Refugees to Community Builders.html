🤝 Article: From Refugees to Community Builders
From Refugees to Community Builders
One family paved the way for Hmong to move to Minnesota
by Laura Yuen · The Minnesota Star Tribune

Bill Hawj, the 49-year-old Uber driver from Roseville, might tell you that he was the first Hmong baby born in Minnesota. Bill's parents, Dang and Shoua Hawj, were the first Hmong refugees to come to the state, an illustrious distinction confirmed by community members and historians. On Dec. 5, 1975, the couple and their 2-year-old son—Bill's older brother, Touvi—arrived in Minnesota, clearing a path for the tens of thousands who followed.

illustrious
(adj) famous and admired because of what you have achieved
ilustre, prestigioso
She comes from an illustrious family of doctors.
refugee
(n) someone who has been forced to leave their country
refugiado/a
The refugees found a new home in Minnesota.
clear a path
(phrasal) to make it easier for others to follow later
abrir camino
Their courage cleared a path for future generations.

The Hawj family is proud of all that their community has produced, from an Olympic gymnast in Suni Lee to the newest mayor of St. Paul, Kaohly Her. Hmong American writers like Kao Kalia Yang have enriched Minnesota's literary canon. Chefs Diane Moua and Yia Vang have invigorated our culinary landscape. "It's not just what they do, politically or economically, but they've stimulated the imagination of American society," said Mai Na Lee, a history professor at the University of Minnesota. But somebody had to be first. It took one family to chart a course that helped change our state's trajectory. "I'm very proud of what my folks were able to accomplish—to bring us here, survive here and then raise us," said Touvi Her, now 52.

canon
(n) all the books recognized as the most important works of literature
canon (literario)
Her novel became part of the modern literary canon.
invigorate
(v) to give new energy or strength to something
revigorizar, renovar
The new chef invigorated the restaurant's menu.
trajectory
(n) the path or direction that events follow over time
trayectoria
The decision changed the trajectory of her career.
folks
(n, informal) parents or family members; people in general
padres, familia (informal)
My folks worked hard to give us a good life.
🧭 How they got here

Touvi was too young to retain any memories of fleeing Laos at the end of the Vietnam War. The Hmong had been crucial allies of the U.S. government, fighting alongside the CIA and rescuing downed American pilots in what has become known as the Secret War in Laos. But the Hmong's alliance with the U.S. would cost them. After Laos fell to the Communists in 1975, members of this ethnic minority were targeted for reprisals. While the U.S. government accepted Vietnamese and Cambodians, it initially refused to open its doors to the Hmong, deeming them "too primitive" to survive here.

retain
(v) to keep or continue to have something, like a memory
conservar, retener
He couldn't retain any memory of the accident.
ally / alliance
(n) a country or person who works together with another for a shared goal
aliado / alianza
The two nations formed a strong alliance.
downed pilot
(n) a pilot whose aircraft has been shot down
piloto derribado
They rescued several downed pilots during the war.
reprisal
(n) something harmful done to punish someone
represalia
The soldiers feared reprisals after the war ended.
deem
(v) to think of something in a particular way
considerar, juzgar
The officials deemed the plan too risky.

The Americans, however, had vastly underestimated the Hmong's ability to adapt. This was a people who had fled persecution in China thousands of years ago and migrated throughout southeast Asia, bending to survive in each place they called home.

underestimate
(v) to think something is smaller, weaker, or less capable than it is
subestimar
Never underestimate how hard she works.
flee (fled)
(v) to run away from danger or persecution
huir
Thousands fled the region during the war.
bend (to survive)
(v, figurative) to adapt or adjust rather than break
adaptarse, doblegarse
Successful people learn to bend without breaking.
🏠 Homesick

The earliest refugees were key to easing the way for later waves of immigrants. When the Hawj family arrived in Minnesota in December 1975, they faced both an unforgiving winter and the emotional challenges of being the first Hmong family here. Dang's wife, Shoua, now 72, remembers those early days living with a sponsor family in Anoka. While Dang was at school all day studying English, Shoua stayed inside her bedroom, looking out the window and sobbing.

unforgiving
(adj) very harsh, severe, showing no mercy
implacable, severo
The unforgiving winter made travel almost impossible.
sponsor family
(n) a family that supports and helps a refugee or immigrant settle
familia patrocinadora
The sponsor family helped them find a home and a job.
sob
(v) to cry loudly, often with deep sadness
sollozar
She sat by the window, sobbing quietly.

After baby Bill was born the following February, Shoua was reminded how far she was from home. Shoua paced, anxious and homesick. She also could not make herself the traditional chicken herb soup that has nourished Hmong moms for centuries. The herbs were nowhere to be found in Minnesota. A nod to his family's initial pangs of homesickness, Dang established two Hmong grocery stores in St. Paul.

pace
(v) to walk back and forth because you are anxious
caminar de un lado a otro (por ansiedad)
He paced the hallway while waiting for news.
nourish
(v) to feed and keep someone healthy and strong
nutrir, alimentar
The soup has nourished families for generations.
pang
(n) a sudden feeling of pain or sadness
punzada (de dolor o tristeza)
She felt a pang of homesickness during the holidays.
a nod to
(phrase) something done as a tribute or reference to another thing
un guiño a, un homenaje a
The store's name is a nod to their hometown.
🌆 A new frontier

As more Southeast Asians made the state their new home, Shoua started working as an interpreter for early childhood education in St. Paul, helping teach other moms how to cook using a stove and bundle up their kids for Minnesota winters. Dang also made sure to lift others up. He helped found what became Lao Family Community of Minnesota, a social services organization, and planned annual gatherings that thrive to this day, including New Year's celebrations and the Hmong International Freedom Festival. He helped found the St. Paul Hmong Alliance Church, one of the largest of its kind in the nation.

interpreter
(n) a person who translates spoken language
intérprete
She worked as an interpreter for new families.
found (an organization)
(v) to start or establish something new
fundar
He founded a community organization in 1980.
thrive
(v) to grow, develop, or succeed well
prosperar, florecer
The tradition still thrives fifty years later.
🙏 Giving thanks

While many community members are thriving, disparities persist. About a fifth of Hmong Minnesotans over 25 lack a high school diploma or its equivalent. It was only when Dang's daughter Risa became an adult that she realized how much her parents helped shape the community. "My dad, he was a big visionary person," she said. "We were so young to understand that at the time." We give thanks for the Hmong who bravely started over in Minnesota. They paid a painful price but showed us just what they could do when given room to try.

disparity
(n) a noticeable and unfair difference between groups
desigualdad, disparidad
Wide disparities in income remain a problem.
equivalent
(n/adj) something equal in value or meaning
equivalente
A GED is the equivalent of a diploma.
visionary
(adj) having clear ideas about what the future should be like
visionario
Her grandfather was a true visionary leader.
room to try
(phrase) the space or opportunity to attempt something
espacio/oportunidad para intentarlo
All they needed was room to try and prove themselves.

✍️ Self-Practice Questions
1. Fill in the blank: The U.S. government initially refused Hmong refugees entry, ______ them "too primitive" to survive.
Answer: deeming — "deem" means to think of or judge something in a particular way.
2. What is the difference between "reprisal" and "pang"? Try to explain in your own words before checking.
Answer: A reprisal is a harmful, often violent action taken to punish someone (e.g., political persecution). A pang is a sudden emotional feeling, like a quick stab of sadness or homesickness — much smaller and personal in scale.
3. Use "underestimate" in a sentence about a time someone misjudged you or your abilities.
Sample answer: My manager underestimated how quickly I could learn the new system, but I finished the training in half the expected time.
4. True or False: The word "trajectory" in the article refers only to the physical path of an airplane.
Answer: False. In this article, "trajectory" is used figuratively to describe the long-term direction or path of the state of Minnesota's development, not a literal flight path.
