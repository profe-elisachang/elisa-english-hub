🎨 Surrounded by Idiots

A simple guide to communicating with everyone

💬 Useful Expressions

I tend to be more…
➜ Suelo ser más…
“I tend to be more analytical when I review an incident report.”
It’s just a difference in style.
➜ Es solo una diferencia de estilo.
“Don’t take it personally. It’s just a difference in style.”
Let me look at this from your side.
➜ Déjame verlo desde tu perspectiva.
“Let me look at this from your side. What worries you most about the new system?”
I want to make sure we’re on the same page.
➜ Quiero asegurarme de que estamos de acuerdo / entendemos lo mismo.
“Before the audit, I want to make sure we’re on the same page.”
Let’s slow down and go over the details.
➜ Vamos más despacio y revisemos los detalles.
“Let’s slow down and go over the details of the new policy.”

🎨 Article

Do you ever wonder why some people are so difficult to work with? In Surrounded by Idiots, Thomas Erikson suggests it may not be because they are “idiots” at all. Instead, people often think and communicate in very different ways. To explain this, Erikson introduces four main styles of behavior that are represented by colors: Red, Yellow, Green and Blue. Each color reflects a certain pattern of behavior that affects how people speak, listen and make decisions.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 1
wonder
verb

to ask yourself a question; to be curious about something

🇲🇽 preguntarse

“I wonder why my team ignores the safety checklist.”

work with
phrasal verb

to deal with or cooperate with someone

🇲🇽 trabajar con / tratar con

“He is hard to work with when a deadline is close.”

instead
adverb

as a different choice or option

🇲🇽 en cambio / en lugar de eso

“Don’t send an email. Call the plant manager instead.”

pattern
noun

a regular way that something happens or is done

🇲🇽 patrón / forma habitual

“HR noticed a pattern in the exit interviews.”

According to Erikson, many conflicts come from assuming that everyone thinks like we do. Reds are direct and oriented toward action. Yellows are energetic and imaginative. Greens are calm and supportive. Blues are analytical and careful.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 2
come from
phrasal verb

to be caused by something; to have as a source

🇲🇽 surgir de / provenir de

“Most IT tickets come from password problems.”

assume
verb

to believe something is true without checking

🇲🇽 suponer

“Never assume the machine is locked out.”

supportive
adjective

helpful and kind to other people

🇲🇽 solidario / que apoya

“My supervisor is very supportive during audits.”

careful
adjective

paying attention to avoid mistakes or danger

🇲🇽 cuidadoso / meticuloso

“Be careful with the chemical storage rules.”

None of these styles are good or bad; they are simply different. When people understand these differences, they can avoid misunderstandings and communicate more clearly.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 3
simply
adverb

only; just; in a plain way

🇲🇽 simplemente

“The delay was simply a scheduling error.”

avoid
verb

to stay away from something; to prevent it

🇲🇽 evitar

“We use checklists to avoid accidents.”

misunderstanding
noun

a failure to understand something correctly

🇲🇽 malentendido

“A misunderstanding about the shift delayed the training.”

The author also explains why clashes happen. A Red may see a Green as slow. A Blue may think a Yellow is careless. But these judgments come from stress, not truth. Erikson encourages readers to look beyond their first reactions and try to understand the intention behind someone’s behavior. This awareness helps people work together with more patience and respect.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 4
clash
noun / verb

a strong disagreement or conflict between people

🇲🇽 choque / enfrentamiento

“There was a clash between IT and HR about data access.”

careless
adjective

not paying enough attention; making mistakes

🇲🇽 descuidado

“A careless mistake on the form caused a delay.”

encourage
verb

to give someone confidence or reasons to do something

🇲🇽 animar a / alentar a

“Our manager encourages us to report near misses.”

look beyond
phrasal verb

to see more than the obvious first impression

🇲🇽 ir más allá de

“Look beyond the tone of the email and read the request.”

The second half of Surrounded by Idiots shows how to apply these ideas. Erikson suggests changes people can make, many of which are essential for clearer communication. For example, Reds may need to slow down and give others more space. Yellows may need to focus on details. Greens could practice speaking up. Blues may need to share their thoughts without over-analyzing everything. These adjustments make conversations more constructive.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 5
slow down
phrasal verb

to go less fast; to reduce speed or pace

🇲🇽 bajar el ritmo / ir más despacio

“Slow down and read the safety instructions again.”

focus on
phrasal verb

to give your main attention to something

🇲🇽 enfocarse en

“Let’s focus on the root cause of the incident.”

speak up
phrasal verb

to say your opinion openly, especially in a group

🇲🇽 atreverse a opinar / alzar la voz

“If you see a risk, please speak up in the meeting.”

over-analyze
verb

to think about something too much, for too long

🇲🇽 analizar en exceso

“Don’t over-analyze the report; send it today.”

Erikson also offers strategies for reducing conflict. One suggestion is to observe how people speak and move. A fast-talking coworker with bold opinions may be a Red, while a quiet listener who avoids arguments may be a Green. When people recognize these patterns, they can modify their approach so it is compatible with others. Instead of reacting in an abrupt or emotional way, they can respond with understanding.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 6
fast-talking

🇲🇽 de habla rápida

“The fast-talking vendor promised a quick installation.”

bold
adjective

confident and not afraid to take risks

🇲🇽 audaz / contundente

“She made a bold proposal to change the onboarding process.”

approach
noun

a way of dealing with a situation or problem

🇲🇽 enfoque / manera de abordar algo

“We need a new approach to training new hires.”

abrupt
adjective

sudden and unfriendly; without warning

🇲🇽 brusco / repentino

“His abrupt answer surprised the whole team.”

The book’s key message is simple: Communication improves when people stop assuming others are wrong and start seeing differences as strengths. By accepting each style, teams become more balanced and friendships become more supportive. Erikson hopes readers will use this framework to create healthier relationships and work environments. With practice, anyone can become more flexible, more patient and less frustrated by those around them.

Vocabulary · Paragraph 7
strengths
noun

🇲🇽 fortalezas

“Each person’s strengths help the project.”

balanced
adjective

🇲🇽 equilibrado

“A balanced team has detail people and big-picture people.”

framework

🇲🇽 marco de referencia

“This framework helps us predict communication problems.”

those around them

“Good leaders listen to those around them.”

🗣️ Discussion Questions

  1. Think of a coworker you find difficult. Which color might they be? What could be the intention behind their behavior?
  2. Erikson says our negative judgments “come from stress, not truth.” Do you agree? Give an example from your workplace (EHS, HR or IT).
  3. Which change would be hardest for you: slowing down, focusing on details, speaking up, or sharing thoughts without over-analyzing? Why?
