👻🔬 Ghosts or Science?

What really explains the supernatural?

Engineer Vic Tandy worked in a laboratory some claimed was haunted. One night Tandy became gripped by fear while he was working alone in the lab. He felt a chill and, out of the corner of his eye, spotted a gray figure. Someone - or something - stood next to him. He turned to face the figure, but it vanished as quickly as it had appeared.

gripped
Completely controlled by a strong feeling.
invadido / dominado"Panic gripped the team during the fire drill."
chill
A sudden cold feeling in the body.
escalofrío"I felt a chill when I entered the server room."
out of the corner of his eye
Seen at the edge of your vision, not directly.
de reojo"She noticed smoke out of the corner of her eye."
vanished
Disappeared suddenly and completely.
se esfumó"The file vanished from the shared drive."

Curious about his "ghost," Tandy investigated. He found that an industrial fan was emitting infrasound waves - sound waves that are too low to hear but still strong enough to feel. They may cause feelings of dread or the sense of a strange presence and can even make the eyes quiver a bit, causing optical illusions. "When we finally switched [the fan] off, ... a huge weight was lifted," Tandy explains. It's important to rule out natural causes before attributing something to the supernatural.

dread
Strong fear about something that may happen.
pavor"He felt dread before the safety inspection."
quiver
To shake with small, fast movements.
temblar / vibrar"Her voice began to quiver during the presentation."
switch off
To stop a machine by turning a switch.
apagar"Please switch off the equipment before cleaning."
rule out
To decide that something is not the cause or possible.
descartar"IT ruled out a cyberattack."

Infrasound waves can't explain every strange experience reported by people, however. The H family was terrified, feeling sure their home was haunted. They felt weak, experienced terrible headaches and heard mysterious footsteps, furniture moving and even voices. They also reported seeing ghosts. In another case, Dr. Rogers, a professor, noticed his children often acted strangely after leaving their home's basement. These families were convinced that their homes were truly haunted.

haunted
Visited by ghosts, or believed to be.
embrujado(a)"Some employees say the old warehouse is haunted."
headaches
Pains in the head.
dolores de cabeza"Strong chemical smells caused headaches."
footsteps
The sound of someone walking.
pasos"The guard heard footsteps in the empty hallway."
basement
A floor of a building below ground level.
sótano"The backup servers are in the basement."

If you experienced strange, unexpected things, you might have been convinced that something supernatural was happening, too. Yet, science can account for the disturbing sounds, sights and physical symptoms experienced by the H and Rogers families. The scary illness and false sights and sounds at the H family's house ceased once a heater, which had been leaking poisonous gas, was fixed. Dr. Rogers' basement had mold. His civil and environmental engineering research team collected data from 27 houses that contained mold. The 13 so-called haunted houses out of the 27 contained the highest levels of mold, which triggered breathing difficulties, feelings of terror and sore eyes that saw dark floating shapes.

account for
To explain the reason for something.
explicar"A faulty sensor accounts for the false alarms."
ceased
Stopped happening (formal).
cesaron / dejaron de"The complaints ceased after the repair."
leaking
Letting liquid or gas escape by accident.
con fuga"A leaking pipe forced us to stop production."
mold
A fuzzy fungus that grows in damp places.
moho"Mold appeared on the office walls."

Spooky sensations can also be induced by a vivid imagination! Psychologist Dr. French of Goldsmiths, University of London studied whether infrasound waves, electromagnetic fields and subjects' imaginations could disrupt brain signals, causing false sights or sounds. He warned subjects of potential "weird experiences" inside the test room and then exposed them to infrasound, electromagnetic activity or nothing at all. In each case, about eight percent of subjects reported feeling terror or other unusual sensations. He concluded that even "the power of suggestion" could cause some participants' imaginations to run wild.

spooky
Strange and a little frightening.
espeluznante"The empty plant at night felt spooky."
warn
To tell someone about a possible danger.
advertir"EHS warned us about the slippery floor."
expose to
To let someone experience something, often harmful.
exponer a"Workers were exposed to loud noise."
run wild
To go out of control.
volar sin control"Rumors ran wild after the announcement."

So while strange phenomena may be attributed to the supernatural, science has often explained them.

phenomena
Plural of "phenomenon": events that can be observed. Say: fuh-NOM-uh-nuh.
fenómenos"Natural phenomena like storms can affect operations."
attribute to
To say that something is caused by something else.
atribuir a"The delay was attributed to a supplier error."
strange
Unusual and hard to understand.
extraño"We noticed a strange noise from the machine."

🗣️ Discussion Questions

  1. Have you ever had a strange experience that seemed to have no explanation? What did you think caused it?
  2. In your workplace, what invisible factors (noise, air quality, lighting, stress) could affect how people feel or perform?
  3. Why do you think people often choose a supernatural explanation before a scientific one? How can we encourage people to "rule out" natural causes first?
